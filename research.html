November 6, 2026 — Complement deposition in MDS marrow

Fattizzo et al., Complement deposition in the bone marrow of patients with myelodysplastic neoplasms. Peer-reviewed open-access letter, Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy, published November 2. The cohort included 44 predominantly low-/intermediate-risk MDS patients; trephines underwent IHC for C3, C4d, IgG and IgM, with clinical/molecular correlation. A five-patient MDS subset plus three controls underwent flow assessment of C3d/IgG deposition, and single-cell RNA sequencing was performed in ten MDS cases versus three healthy donors. C3/C4d deposition was detected in 96%/89%; higher-grade deposition correlated with lower counts, transfusion dependence and reduced ESA response. MDS erythroid cells showed lower CD55/CD59, while CD16-positive monocytes showed increased C1QA/C1QB, factor D, properdin, FCGR and MHC-II expression. PMID 42967906; DOI 10.1038/s41392-026-03052-1. Limitations: small, treatment-heterogeneous, predominantly lower-risk cohort; semi-quantitative IHC without an independent trephine validation cohort; flow and single-cell comparisons were very small; associations do not establish complement-mediated causality or therapeutic utility. Project implication: complement is a plausible exploratory tissue axis, but C3/C4d should be piloted on locally fixed/decalcified FFPE trephines before panel expansion. Record deposition by compartment (serous/background, erythroid islands, megakaryocytes and mononuclear cells), correlate with hemoglobin, transfusion dependence, ESA exposure/response, fibrosis, IPSS-M and mutations, and validate digital scores against blinded ordinal review.

Feasible pilot: on a small tissue microarray or 20–30 representative whole sections, test C3 and C4d staining robustness after local decalcification and quantify compartment-specific deposition using pathologist-defined annotations. Add IgG/IgM only if background and specificity are acceptable. This is hypothesis-generating and should not displace the planned CD163/CD68/checkpoint/S100A8/A9 core panel without reproducible technical performance.

October 30, 2026 — Newly indexed lymphoma pathology studies

Wang et al., High-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration. Peer-reviewed retrospective study, Histopathology, published October 18. Among 92 high-grade/DLBCL cases tested by 11q FISH, 27 had an 11q aberration; 74% had Burkitt-like/blastoid morphology, 96% a germinal-center phenotype, all tested cases expressed MYC, and 89% were BCL2-negative. All 21 assessed marrows were negative at diagnosis; 24 patients achieved complete remission. PMID 42893696; DOI 10.1111/his.70303. Limitation: single-center, selected for prior 11q FISH, with molecular testing in 14 cases. Implication: in MYC-positive/BCL2-negative Burkitt-like or blastoid lymphomas without MYC rearrangement, preserve tissue for 11q-pattern testing and document morphology separately from molecular classification.

Sethi et al., Low-grade B-cell lymphomas with primary osseous presentation. Peer-reviewed single-center retrospective series, American Journal of Surgical Pathology, published October 19. Thirty-two bone-only cases comprised FL 69%, MZL 19%, LPL 6%, and CLL/SLL 6%; 78% were multifocal, usually pelvis/spine. Twelve of 27 patients with more than six months of follow-up progressed or transformed, including three FL-to-DLBCL transformations, with no deaths reported. PMID 42894406; DOI 10.1097/PAS.0000000000002631. Limitation: 32 cases from a tertiary referral center with heterogeneous subtypes and follow-up. Implication: osseous low-grade lymphoma should be classified using the same integrated morphology/IHC/FISH/molecular framework as nodal disease and not presumed aggressive from site alone.

Feasible project idea (proposed): retrospective review of lymphoma presenting in bone or soft tissue, correlating biopsy morphology, flow/IHC, FISH/NGS, imaging distribution, diagnostic category, repeat-biopsy rate, and transformation. Minimum requirement: pathology archive plus radiology and follow-up data. Main feasibility issue: small numbers and referral-selection bias; novelty should be checked against regional datasets before launch.

November 6, 2026 — Newly indexed lymphoma pathology study

Li et al., Bone-marrow BCL2 expression by flow cytometry in follicular lymphoma. Peer-reviewed cohort study, Cytometry Part B: Clinical Cytometry, published October 30. The investigators studied 265 patients with FL (230 newly diagnosed; 35 relapsed/refractory) across BD and Beckman instrument cohorts, assessing intracellular BCL2 mean fluorescence intensity, BCL2-positive percentage, and the BCL2:CD200 ratio. Flow-detectable marrow FL showed CD10 positivity, dim CD19/CD20/CD22, absent CD200, and higher BCL2 measures; BCL2-positive percentage had the best discrimination. Findings were directionally consistent across both platforms. PMID 42960882; DOI 10.1002/cyto.b.70089. Limitations: the non-FL comparator comprised only 13 cases (CLL 6, MZL 4, MCL 3); thresholds and MFI are platform/antibody dependent; the prognostic associations were exploratory and internally discordant (marrow-positive status predicted shorter PFS, whereas higher BCL2 among positive cases correlated with longer PFS). Implication: BCL2 may be a useful adjunct in a marrow flow panel but cannot replace morphology, core-biopsy IHC, clonality, or IGH::BCL2 testing.

Feasible project idea (proposed): retrospectively validate intracellular BCL2 percentage and BCL2:CD200 ratio in marrow staging samples from known FL, with blinded comparison against core-biopsy involvement and an indolent B-cell lymphoma control group. Minimum requirements are archived flow list-mode files with stable antibody clones/instrument settings, paired trephine diagnosis, and ideally IGH::BCL2 status. Candidate endpoints are sensitivity/specificity, added yield in morphologically subtle disease, and inter-platform reproducibility. Main feasibility issue: historical panels may lack intracellular BCL2 or sufficiently standardized acquisition; novelty and local case volume require confirmation.

November 13, 2026 — Newly published DLBCL biomarker study

Gupta et al., Clinicopathological associations and prognostic significance of EZH2, H3K27me3, pRB, p53 and p16 expression in DLBCL. Peer-reviewed retrospective study, Journal of Pathology and Translational Medicine, published online November 13. The authors reviewed 131 primary nodal DLBCL, NOS cases from a 3.5-year archive and applied Hans classification plus IHC for EZH2, H3K27me3, pRB, p16 and p53. EZH2 was expressed in 59.5% and associated with GCB phenotype, CD10 and p53; pRB was more frequent in the ABC-like group. None of the studied IHC markers produced a clinically useful survival cutoff: ROC areas for overall survival were 0.39–0.54, and only age over 60 and ECOG 2 or greater remained independently prognostic. DOI 10.4132/jptm.2026.09.14. Limitations: single-center retrospective cohort; 94 of 225 screened cases were excluded for tissue or clinical-data limitations; primary extranodal and transformed cases were excluded; FISH exclusion of double-/triple-hit disease was performed only when clinically or morphologically suspected; Hans IHC is an imperfect surrogate for gene-expression COO. Implication: EZH2/H3K27me3/pRB/p16/p53 IHC should not be adopted as an isolated prognostic panel. The negative result argues that any local DLBCL biomarker project should use molecularly defined groups and prespecified endpoints rather than optimizing retrospective IHC cutoffs.

October 30, 2026 — Newly indexed diagnostic case report

Centoducatte et al., Extracavitary primary effusion lymphoma in an HIV-negative patient. Peer-reviewed case report, EJHaem, published October 22 and newly indexed after the prior review. A gastric plasmablastic infiltrate showed strong CD30, MUM1, weak focal CD3, EBER positivity, and HHV8/LANA-1 positivity—an extracavitary PEL phenotype despite HIV-negative status and aberrant T-cell-marker expression. PMID 42923817; DOI 10.1002/jha2.70443. Practical lesson: for a plasmablastic, CD30-positive extranodal lymphoma with weak aberrant CD3, include HHV8/LANA-1 and EBER before assigning plasmablastic lymphoma or T-cell lineage. Limitation: single case; PubMed had no abstract, and interpretation was based on the accessible full-text figure and indexed article.

November 6, 2026 — Newly indexed diagnostic case reports

Yamada et al., Localized high-grade B-cell lymphoma with 11q aberration and prolonged remission. Peer-reviewed case report, Journal of Clinical and Experimental Hematopathology, published October 30. A young woman had a completely resected localized, diffuse high-grade lymphoma with starry-sky morphology, high MYC expression, high proliferation, GCB phenotype, and no MYC rearrangement. Subsequent FISH showed 11q23.3 gain with 11q24.3 loss, establishing HGBCL with 11q aberration; she remained in remission for 8.5 years after limited rituximab exposure without intensive chemotherapy. PMID 42968287; DOI 10.3960/jslrt.26047. Practical lesson: in a MYC-rearrangement-negative Burkitt-like/GCB lymphoma, test for the characteristic 11q gain/loss pattern rather than defaulting to DLBCL, NOS. Limitation: a single localized case cannot define treatment or prognosis.

Alhalaseh et al., Cutaneous presentation of nodal TFH-cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type, with EBV-positive B-cell lymphoproliferation. Peer-reviewed diagnostic case/answer, American Journal of Dermatopathology, published November 1. Recurrent subcutaneous nodules in a 72-year-old man were initially interpreted as a reactive histiocytic process. A later punch biopsy showed a lymphohistiocytic/plasmacytic infiltrate with atypical cells; the T-cell population expressed CD3, CD4, PD-1, ICOS and CXCL13 with variable CD10/BCL6, while an associated EBV-positive, kappa-restricted B-cell proliferation was present; T- and B-cell clonality studies were positive. PMID 42900688; DOI 10.1097/DAD.0000000000003386. Practical lesson: progressive cutaneous lymphohistiocytic infiltrates merit repeat/deeper sampling, a multi-marker TFH panel, EBER localization, and clonality; the EBV-positive B-cell component should not obscure the TFH neoplasm. Limitation: single case; detailed diagnostic findings were verified from the associated conference abstract because the indexed journal item has no abstract.

November 13, 2026 — Newly indexed diagnostic case report

Zheng et al., Assay-aware flow-cytometric interpretation of relapsed B-ALL after sequential CD19- and CD22-directed therapy. Peer-reviewed case report, Medicine, published November 6 and indexed after the previous review. At relapse after blinatumomab, blasts retained cytoplasmic CD19 and CD79a but lacked surface CD19, expressed weak surface CD22 and aberrant CD13/CD33, and were negative for cytoplasmic MPO, CD14 and CD64—supporting B-ALL rather than mixed-phenotype acute leukemia. After inotuzumab, a 0.007% abnormal population (approximately 30 events) was above the assay limit of detection but below its limit of quantification; surface CD22 was not demonstrable in that low-event population. PMID 42994847; DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000051427. Practical lesson: separate lineage assignment from therapeutic-target assessment: cytoplasmic CD19 can support B-lineage but does not establish surface CD19 availability, and CD13/CD33 alone do not establish myeloid lineage. Populations above detection but below quantification should be reported qualitatively with event count and assay limits. Limitation: single case with incomplete treatment-history reconstruction; one low-event post-treatment sample cannot prove antigen loss, lineage switch, treatment causality or durable clearance.
