Documentation rule

Every note should explicitly state indication, procedure type, volume, replacement fluid, access, calcium/anticoagulation, pre/post vitals, labs, fluid balance, complications, and next plan.

1. Documentation workflow

On-call use

Consult note purpose

Establish whether apheresis is indicated, document risk-benefit discussion and consent, define access and replacement fluid, and set a treatment endpoint.

Procedure note purpose

Document what was actually done: volume exchanged, product used, access performance, calcium given, vitals, labs, fluid balance, complications, and next session.

Note typeMust include
Initial consultationReason for consult, relevant history, clinical indication, ASFA category if useful, labs, access assessment, consent, planned prescription, monitoring, endpoint.
Daily procedure noteDiagnosis, procedure number, exchange/replacement details, access, pre/post vitals, labs, fluid balance, complications, next plan.
Complication noteEvent timing, procedure status, symptoms/signs, actions taken, response, communication with primary team, decision to continue/stop/retry.

Copy-ready

Use these as starting points. Replace bracketed text and adjust to local policy, current ASFA guidance, and patient-specific details.

TPE consultation - suspected acquired TTP

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE / APHERESIS CONSULTATION NOTE

Reason for consultation:
Therapeutic plasma exchange for suspected acquired thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP).

History / clinical context:
[Age]-year-old [sex] with [presentation: thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, neurologic symptoms, renal dysfunction, fever, other]. The apheresis service was consulted for urgent evaluation for TPE.

Pertinent findings:
Platelets: [ ] x 10^9/L
Hemoglobin: [ ] g/L
LDH: [ ]
Bilirubin: [ ]
Haptoglobin: [ ]
Creatinine: [ ]
Peripheral smear: [schistocytes present / pending / not seen]
Coagulation studies: [PT/INR/PTT/fibrinogen]
ADAMTS13: [sent before TPE / pending / not yet sent]

Assessment:
The clinical and laboratory findings are concerning for TTP. Given the high morbidity and mortality of untreated acquired TTP, urgent TPE is indicated and should not be delayed while awaiting ADAMTS13 results when clinical suspicion is high.

Plan:
1. Obtain informed consent for therapeutic plasma exchange and blood product exposure.
2. Draw ADAMTS13 activity/inhibitor before first exchange if this will not delay treatment.
3. Start TPE urgently: exchange 1.0 to 1.5 total plasma volumes daily.
4. Replacement fluid: donor plasma.
5. Access: [peripheral access / temporary apheresis or dialysis catheter].
6. Monitor CBC, platelet count, LDH, bilirubin, hemolysis markers, electrolytes including ionized calcium, and coagulation studies as clinically indicated.
7. Coordinate disease-directed therapy with hematology, including corticosteroids and consideration of caplacizumab and rituximab according to local protocol.
8. Continue daily TPE until platelet count and hemolysis have clearly improved, then stop/taper according to institutional TTP protocol and clinical response.

TPE consultation - Guillain-Barre syndrome / immune neuropathy

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE / APHERESIS CONSULTATION NOTE

Reason for consultation:
Therapeutic plasma exchange for [Guillain-Barre syndrome / CIDP exacerbation / myasthenia gravis crisis / other immune-mediated neurologic disorder].

History / clinical context:
[Age]-year-old [sex] with [diagnosis] presenting with [weakness, sensory symptoms, bulbar symptoms, respiratory involvement, progression despite IVIG/steroids, other]. Neurology has requested TPE.

Pertinent findings:
Neurologic status: [summary]
Respiratory status: [stable / declining / ventilated / FVC/NIF if available]
Prior therapy: [IVIG / steroids / none / other]
CBC: [ ]
Creatinine/electrolytes/calcium: [ ]
Coagulation/fibrinogen if relevant: [ ]
Access assessment: [ ]

Assessment:
The clinical scenario is compatible with an immune-mediated neurologic disorder for which TPE is an accepted treatment option. TPE is intended to remove pathogenic circulating antibodies or immune mediators and should be coordinated with neurology and ICU/respiratory monitoring when needed.

Plan:
1. Obtain informed consent for TPE.
2. Planned course: [5] exchanges, typically every other day, with reassessment based on clinical response.
3. Exchange volume: approximately 1.0 total plasma volume per session.
4. Replacement fluid: 5% albumin unless plasma is specifically indicated.
5. Access: [peripheral / central].
6. Calcium replacement and citrate monitoring per protocol.
7. Monitor neurologic and respiratory status closely; coordinate with neurology regarding endpoint and concurrent therapy.

TPE consultation - cryoglobulinemia / cryoglobulinemic vasculitis

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE / APHERESIS CONSULTATION NOTE

Reason for consultation:
Therapeutic plasma exchange for severe cryoglobulinemia / cryoglobulinemic vasculitis.

History / clinical context:
[Age]-year-old [sex] with [HCV / autoimmune disease / lymphoproliferative disorder / other] and evidence of severe cryoglobulinemia with [renal involvement, skin vasculitis, neuropathy, hyperviscosity, other].

Pertinent findings:
Cryoglobulin/cryocrit: [ ]
Complement levels: [ ]
Renal function: creatinine [ ], proteinuria [ ]
Biopsy findings if available: [ ]
CBC/coagulation/fibrinogen: [ ]
Volume status/comorbidities: [CHF/renal failure/edema/other]

Assessment:
The patient has severe cryoglobulinemia with organ-threatening involvement. TPE is reasonable as a rapid temporizing therapy to remove circulating cryoglobulins while definitive disease-directed therapy is initiated.

Plan:
1. Obtain informed consent for TPE.
2. Planned course: [5 to 7] procedures, frequency [daily / every other day] depending on severity and response.
3. Exchange volume: approximately 1.0 total plasma volume per session.
4. Replacement fluid: 5% albumin, with plasma added only if clinically indicated for bleeding/coagulopathy.
5. Use isovolemic or carefully controlled fluid balance if volume overload risk is present.
6. Calcium replacement per citrate protocol.
7. Monitor renal function, urine protein, cryoglobulin/cryocrit if used locally, symptoms, fibrinogen, and access status.
8. Coordinate antiviral, immunosuppressive, or hematologic therapy with the primary team.

TPE consultation - catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE / APHERESIS CONSULTATION NOTE

Reason for consultation:
Therapeutic plasma exchange for suspected catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS) or severe antibody-mediated hypercoagulability.

History / clinical context:
[Age]-year-old [sex] with [recurrent thrombosis / multiorgan thrombosis / pulmonary embolism / DVT / renal/CNS/skin involvement] despite [anticoagulation or other therapy]. Hematology/ICU has requested TPE.

Pertinent findings:
Thrombotic events: [ ]
Antiphospholipid testing: [LA/anticardiolipin/beta-2 glycoprotein results or pending]
Anticoagulation: [heparin/argatroban/warfarin/DOAC]
Platelets: [ ]
PT/INR/PTT/fibrinogen: [ ]
Renal/liver function: [ ]
Bleeding risk: [ ]

Assessment:
The clinical scenario is concerning for CAPS or severe antibody-mediated thrombotic disease. TPE may be used as part of multimodal therapy together with anticoagulation, corticosteroids, and other immunomodulatory therapy as directed by hematology/rheumatology/ICU.

Plan:
1. Obtain informed consent for TPE and blood product exposure.
2. Planned course: [5] procedures with daily reassessment.
3. Exchange volume: 1.0 to 1.5 total plasma volumes.
4. Replacement fluid: [plasma / albumin plus plasma], selected based on bleeding risk, fibrinogen, anticoagulation, and local protocol.
5. Monitor CBC, coagulation profile, fibrinogen, electrolytes including calcium, and organ-specific markers.
6. Coordinate anticoagulation and immunosuppressive therapy with hematology and the primary team.

RBC exchange consultation - sickle cell disease / acute chest syndrome

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE / APHERESIS CONSULTATION NOTE

Reason for consultation:
Red blood cell exchange for sickle cell disease with [acute stroke / acute chest syndrome / severe vaso-occlusive complication / perioperative indication / other].

History / clinical context:
[Age]-year-old [sex] with [HbSS / HbSC / other sickle genotype] presenting with [clinical syndrome]. The apheresis service was consulted for RBC exchange.

Pertinent findings:
Hemoglobin: [ ] g/L
Hematocrit: [ ]
Platelets: [ ]
HbS/HbC or electrophoresis: [pre-exchange result or pending]
Oxygen requirement / chest imaging / neurologic findings: [ ]
Antibody history / phenotype or genotype: [ ]
Access assessment: [ ]

Assessment:
The clinical indication supports urgent RBC exchange to reduce the fraction of sickling red cells and improve oxygen-carrying capacity while minimizing viscosity risk.

Plan:
1. Obtain consent for RBC exchange and RBC transfusion.
2. Arrange antigen-matched RBC units according to local sickle cell transfusion policy, including Rh and Kell matching and additional matching based on antibody history/genotype where available.
3. Perform automated RBC exchange using [number] units RBC, targeting [post-Hct] and [HbS target] as clinically appropriate.
4. Obtain pre- and post-exchange hemoglobin electrophoresis/HbS quantification.
5. Monitor CBC, calcium, vital signs, transfusion reaction symptoms, and fluid balance.
6. Coordinate ongoing sickle cell management with hematology and the primary team.

Immediate management:

Response:

APHERESIS EVENT NOTE - CITRATE TOXICITY / HYPOCALCEMIA

Event:
During [TPE/RBC exchange], the patient developed symptoms consistent with citrate-related hypocalcemia: [perioral tingling / paresthesia / metallic taste / cramps / carpopedal spasm / nausea / QT prolongation / arrhythmia / seizure].

Assessment:
Ionized calcium was [ ] if available. Symptoms were [mild/moderate/severe].

Management:
The procedure was [continued with intervention / slowed / paused / stopped]. Calcium [gluconate/chloride] [dose] was administered [IV/oral] according to protocol. Citrate exposure/AC ratio was adjusted if applicable.

Response:
Symptoms [resolved/improved/persisted] after [time].

Plan:
For future procedures, use prophylactic calcium, monitor ionized calcium, consider slower citrate exposure, and reassess anticoagulation strategy in high-risk patients.

Allergic reaction during plasma replacement

APHERESIS EVENT NOTE - ALLERGIC REACTION

Event:
During TPE with [plasma/albumin] replacement, the patient developed [urticaria/pruritus/flushing/rigors/dyspnea/wheezing/hypotension/angioedema].

Assessment:
Severity was [mild/moderate/severe/anaphylaxis]. Vital signs: BP [ ], HR [ ], O2 sat [ ].

Management:
The procedure was [paused/stopped]. The patient received [diphenhydramine/acetaminophen/steroid/epinephrine/oxygen/IV fluids] as clinically indicated. Blood bank/transfusion reaction protocol was followed if plasma product reaction was suspected.

Response:
Symptoms [resolved/improved/persisted].

Plan:
For future procedures, consider premedication, alternative plasma product if available, albumin replacement if clinically acceptable, and closer monitoring. Severe reactions require discussion with the apheresis physician and primary team before further procedures.

5. Order sets

Operational

TPE with plasma replacement

  • Therapeutic plasma exchange: [date/time], exchange [ ] mL or [ ] TPV.
  • Replacement fluid: plasma [ ] mL or [ ] units/liters for TPE.
  • Calcium gluconate: [3 to 5 g] IV on call or per protocol.
  • CBC, differential, platelet count; BMP/electrolytes; ionized calcium.
  • LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin for TTP/TMA monitoring.
  • PT/INR, PTT, fibrinogen if bleeding risk or repeated procedures.
  • ADAMTS13 before first TPE if suspected TTP and not already sent.
  • Access order and post-procedure access bleeding check.

TPE with albumin replacement

  • Therapeutic plasma exchange: [date/time], exchange [ ] mL or [ ] TPV.
  • Replacement fluid: 5% albumin [ ] mL at bedside for apheresis.
  • Calcium gluconate: [3 to 5 g] IV on call or per protocol.
  • CBC, BMP/electrolytes, ionized calcium.
  • Fibrinogen and coagulation studies if daily/repeated exchange or procedure planned.
  • Vital signs per apheresis protocol.
  • Access order and post-procedure access assessment.
RBC exchange orders: include RBC product order, antigen-matching requirements, pre- and post-exchange hemoglobin electrophoresis/HbS quantification, CBC, ionized calcium, and access order.
