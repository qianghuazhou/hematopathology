Marker Cards

ALK lymphoma / predictive

Use

  • Diagnose ALK-positive anaplastic large cell lymphoma.
  • Support ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma and rare ALK-positive histiocytosis.
  • Predictive biomarker for ALK inhibitor eligibility in appropriate tumors.
PatternCytoplasmic in most fusions; nuclear + cytoplasmic pattern strongly suggests NPM1::ALK.
NormalRestricted CNS/peripheral nervous system and testis; essentially absent in normal lymphoid tissue.
Positive diseaseALK+ ALCL, ALK+ LBCL, ALK+ histiocytosis; many non-heme tumors including IMT and lung adenocarcinoma.
Pitfall: staining pattern varies by fusion partner. Interpret with CD30, EMA, CD3, cytotoxic markers, PAX5/CD20 and morphology.
CD3 T / NK lineage

Use

  • Most specific routine T-cell marker; confirms T-cell lineage in benign and malignant processes.
  • IHC detects surface and cytoplasmic CD3; NK-cell lesions may be cytoplasmic CD3 positive by IHC but surface CD3 negative by flow.
  • cCD3 supports T-lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma.
NormalThymocytes, peripheral T cells, cytoplasmic CD3 in NK cells; nonspecific faint cytoplasmic staining may occur in plasma cells/macrophages.
Positive diseaseMost T-cell lymphomas, T-ALL/LBL, NK/T lymphoma, reactive T-cell rosettes in NLPHL.
Negative/variableReed-Sternberg cells in CHL negative; LP cells in NLPHL negative. CD3 may be lost/dim in MF, ALCL, AITL and some NK processes.
Pearl: loss of CD3 does not exclude mature T-cell lymphoma. Always compare with CD2, CD5, CD7, CD4/CD8 and TCR markers.
CD15 Hodgkin / granulocytic

Use

  • Classic Hodgkin lymphoma marker used with CD30, PAX5 and CD45.
  • Helps separate CHL from NLPHL and ALCL.
  • Granulocyte marker; may support myeloid differentiation in AML/myeloid sarcoma.
NormalNeutrophils, eosinophils, activated B/T cells, variable monocytes/basophils; Paneth cells and renal proximal tubules.
Positive diseaseCHL usually positive; subset AML, myeloid sarcoma, PTCL, rare B-cell lymphomas and rare ALCL.
NegativeLP cells in NLPHL, most DLBCL, hairy cell leukemia, mastocytosis, LCH.
Pitfall: EBV/infectious mononucleosis can show Reed-Sternberg-like CD15+ cells. CD15 positivity alone is not diagnostic of CHL.
CD20 B-cell / therapeutic

Use

  • Workhorse pan-B-cell marker in reactive and neoplastic lymphoid tissues.
  • Highlights architecture and extent of B-cell infiltrates.
  • Therapeutic target; document expression before/after anti-CD20 therapy.
NormalMature B cells; late hematogones acquire CD20. Plasma cells are negative.
Positive diseaseMost mature B-cell lymphomas; variable B-ALL/LBL; NLPHL usually positive; CHL minority weak/variable.
Negative/variablePost-rituximab B-cell lymphomas may lose CD20. Most plasma cell neoplasms negative.
Pearl: CD20-negative does not exclude B-cell lymphoma. Use PAX5, CD79a and CD19, especially after rituximab or plasmacytic differentiation.
CD30 activation / targeted therapy

Use

  • Essential for CHL, ALCL and lymphomatoid papulosis.
  • Evaluate CD30 expression in T-cell lymphomas and relapsed/refractory DLBCL or PMBL when therapy is relevant.
  • Report any degree of tumor-cell CD30 expression when clinically relevant.
PatternStrong membranous and Golgi in CHL/ALCL; scattered activated immunoblasts may be positive.
NormalTransient activated B, T and NK cells; rare circulating activated cells.
Positive diseaseCHL, ALCL, primary cutaneous ALCL, BIA-ALCL, LyP, PMBL, gray zone lymphoma, EBV+ DLBCL, PEL, EBV mucocutaneous ulcer, MF with LCT.
Pitfall: reactive immunoblasts are often CD30 positive. Estimate neoplastic-cell staining separately from background cells.
CD45 / LCA hematolymphoid origin

Use

  • Confirm hematolymphoid nature of tumors.
  • Distinguish CHL from NLPHL and most nonhematopoietic malignancies.
  • Highlight inflammatory infiltrates.
NormalMost leukocytes including granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes/histiocytes, mast cells and plasma cells; not RBCs, megakaryocytes or platelets.
Positive diseaseMost B/T lymphomas, leukemias, histiocytic tumors, mast cell disorders, BPDCN, NLPHL LP cells.
Negative/variableCHL Reed-Sternberg cells negative; ALCL, plasma cell neoplasms and ALL can be weak/negative.
Pearl: CHL is typically CD45−/CD30+/CD15+/weak PAX5+. NLPHL is CD45+/CD20+/PAX5+/CD30−/CD15−.
EBER ISH / LMP1 EBV

Use

  • EBER ISH is the preferred test for latent EBV infection.
  • Classify EBV-associated B-cell, T/NK-cell, Hodgkin and immunodeficiency-associated lymphoproliferative disorders.
  • LMP1 IHC is supportive but less sensitive than EBER.
EBER patternStrong nuclear signal only.
LMP1 patternCytoplasmic ± membranous.
Positive diseaseExtranodal NK/T lymphoma, aggressive NK leukemia, EBV+ DLBCL, PTLD, methotrexate-associated LPD, EBV mucocutaneous ulcer, subset CHL, PBL/PEL subset.
Pitfall: scattered EBER+ bystander cells can occur. Report whether positivity is in tumor cells or background lymphocytes.
PAX5 / BSAP B-cell lineage

Use

  • Best nuclear marker of B-cell lineage from pro-B to mature B cells.
  • Very useful in CD20-negative/post-rituximab B-cell lymphomas.
  • Weak PAX5 supports CHL; strong PAX5 supports most other B-cell neoplasms and NLPHL.
NormalPro-B, pre-B and mature B cells; lost with terminal plasma cell differentiation.
Positive diseaseMost B-cell lymphomas, B-ALL/LBL, NLPHL, weak CHL, PMBL.
Negative/exceptionPlasma cell neoplasms and plasmacytic component of B-cell lymphomas negative. Rare t(8;21) AML and neuroendocrine tumors may be positive.
Pearl: In Hodgkin workup, intensity matters: CHL is weak PAX5, while NLPHL is strong PAX5/CD20/CD45 positive.
BCL6 germinal center / TFH

Use

  • Marker of germinal center B-cell differentiation; useful with CD10 and BCL2 in follicular lymphoma workup.
  • Used with CD10 and MUM1 for DLBCL cell-of-origin algorithms.
  • TFH marker used with PD1, ICOS, CXCL13 and CD10 for AITL/nodal TFH lymphomas.
PatternNuclear staining.
NormalGerminal center B cells and follicular helper T cells.
Positive diseaseFollicular lymphoma, germinal center subtype DLBCL, Burkitt lymphoma, NLPHL, AITL/nodal TFH lymphomas.
Usually negativePost-germinal center B cells/plasma cells, MALT/nodal marginal zone lymphoma outside colonized follicles, mantle cell lymphoma, most CHL.
Pitfall: BCL6 positivity in colonized follicles does not by itself diagnose follicular lymphoma. Interpret with CD10, BCL2, follicular dendritic meshworks, light chains and architecture.
CD5 T-cell / CD5+ B-cell lymphoma

Use

  • Pan-T-cell marker, normally expressed on most mature T cells.
  • Key marker for CD5-positive B-cell lymphomas, especially CLL/SLL and mantle cell lymphoma.
  • CD5+ DLBCL should prompt exclusion of pleomorphic/blastoid mantle cell lymphoma with cyclin D1, SOX11 and CCND1 testing.
NormalMost mature T cells; small subset of normal B cells.
Positive diseaseMost T-cell lymphomas/leukemias, T-ALL, CLL/SLL, mantle cell lymphoma, subset DLBCL, subset LPL/SMZL/HCL.
Usually negativeFollicular lymphoma, most marginal zone lymphoma/MALT lymphoma, most non-thymic carcinomas; ALCL often negative but variable.
Pearl: CD5 loss in T-cell lymphoma can be diagnostically important. In B-cell lesions, interpret CD5 with CD23, LEF1, cyclin D1 and SOX11.
CD10 / CALLA germinal center / precursor B

Use

  • Marker of germinal center differentiation; useful for follicular lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma and GCB-type DLBCL.
  • Common acute lymphoblastic leukemia antigen in B-ALL; CD10-negative infant/pro-B ALL is associated with KMT2A rearrangement.
  • Helps separate B lymphoblasts from hematogones when used in a broader flow/IHC panel.
NormalGerminal center B cells, early B-cell precursors/hematogones; also non-heme tissues such as renal tubular brush border and stromal/myoepithelial cells.
Positive diseaseFollicular lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma, subset DLBCL, B-ALL/LBL, subset AITL/TFH lymphomas.
Usually negativeCLL/SLL, mantle cell lymphoma, marginal zone lymphoma, lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma, most plasma cell neoplasms.
Pitfall: CD10 is not lymphoma-specific and has many non-heme uses. In lymph nodes, interpret with BCL6, BCL2, CD20/PAX5, Ki-67 and architecture.
CD34 blasts / vessels

Use

  • Identify and estimate blasts in bone marrow cores, especially hypoplastic marrows.
  • Help distinguish MDS/acute leukemia with increased CD34+ blasts from aplastic anemia with low/absent CD34+ progenitors.
  • Also highlights vascular endothelium and can assess microvessels; not all myeloblasts are CD34 positive.
PatternMembranous/cytoplasmic in blasts and endothelial cells.
NormalHematopoietic progenitors/early hematogones, vascular endothelium, some megakaryocytes and stromal cells.
Positive diseaseAML subset, B-ALL/LBL, MDS blasts, transient myeloproliferative disorder blasts; numerous non-heme vascular/spindle cell tumors.
Usually negativeMany mature lymphoid neoplasms; some AML/myeloid sarcomas can be CD34 negative.
Pearl: In marrow, count only appropriate blast morphology/distribution; endothelial cells are internal positive controls and should not be counted as blasts.
CD68 histiocytic / monocytic

Use

  • Highlights macrophages/histiocytes and supports histiocytic differentiation.
  • Useful in histiocytic sarcoma, myeloid/monocytic sarcoma and monocytic differentiation in AML.
  • PGM1 is more specific for monocyte/macrophage lineage than KP1, which is broader and more cross-reactive.
PatternCytoplasmic granular/dot-like staining; clone-dependent.
NormalMacrophages, tissue histiocytes, osteoclasts, Kupffer cells, microglia; lower/variable staining in other myeloid cells and activated lymphocytes depending on clone.
Positive diseaseHistiocytic sarcoma, Rosai-Dorfman disease, LCH, Erdheim-Chester disease, Kikuchi disease, AML/myeloid sarcoma, CMML/CML marrow histiocytes, hairy cell leukemia subset.
Usually negative/limitedALCL usually negative; many sarcomas show variable nonspecific KP1 staining.
Pitfall: CD68 alone is not specific. For histiocytic sarcoma, require a panel such as CD68, CD163, CD4 and lysozyme plus exclusion of mimics.
CD117 / c-KIT blasts / mast cells

Use

  • Estimate immature myeloid cells/blasts in marrow together with CD34 and MPO.
  • Highlight mast cells in mastocytosis workup; use with tryptase, CD25, CD2 and CD30.
  • Non-heme use: supports GIST with DOG1 and CD34.
PatternMembranous and/or cytoplasmic depending on cell type and antibody.
NormalHematopoietic progenitors, mast cells, interstitial cells of Cajal, melanocytes, germ cells and some epithelia.
Positive diseaseAML subset, mast cell disease, GIST, seminoma/dysgerminoma, melanoma and other non-heme tumors.
Usually negativeMany lymphoid neoplasms; CD117 positivity does not establish myeloid lineage alone.
Pearl: CD117 highlights both blasts and mast cells; distinguish by morphology, distribution and tryptase/CD25/CD2/CD30.
E-cadherin erythroid / epithelial adhesion

Use

  • In bone marrow, highlights immature erythroid precursors and supports erythroid differentiation, including pure erythroid leukemia.
  • Non-heme use: distinguishes ductal from lobular breast lesions and helps evaluate tumors with discohesive growth.
PatternMembranous in epithelial cells; membranous staining in erythroid precursors.
NormalMost epithelial cells and hematopoietic erythroid precursors.
Positive diseaseErythroid proliferations/pure erythroid leukemia; most ductal carcinomas and many carcinomas.
Negative/lostLobular breast carcinoma, diffuse-type gastric carcinoma subset, solid pseudopapillary neoplasm of pancreas, plasmacytoid urothelial carcinoma subset.
Pitfall: In breast pathology, morphology takes precedence because aberrant E-cadherin staining can occur. In marrow, interpret with glycophorin/hemoglobin A when needed.
MPO / Myeloperoxidase myeloid lineage

Use

  • Most useful tissue marker for granulocytic/myeloid differentiation in AML and myeloid sarcoma.
  • Part of initial marrow screening with CD34, CD117, CD61 and erythroid markers.
  • Helps AML subtyping; flow/cytochemistry may be more sensitive in some contexts.
PatternDiffuse granular cytoplasmic staining reflecting primary granules.
NormalNeutrophils, eosinophils, myeloid precursors/myeloblasts; monocytes/histiocytes variable and usually less granular.
Positive diseaseAML with granulocytic differentiation, APL, AML with maturation, AML with inv(16), myeloid sarcoma, leukemia cutis.
Usually negativeALL, acute monoblastic/monocytic leukemia often negative/weak, acute megakaryoblastic leukemia, pure erythroid leukemia, BPDCN, histiocytic sarcoma, systemic mastocytosis.
Pitfall: Rare ALL can show isolated MPO by IHC/flow; do not classify lineage from MPO alone if the overall phenotype is discordant.
TdT lymphoblast / thymocyte

Use

  • Identifies immature lymphoid precursors in B-ALL/LBL and T-ALL/LBL.
  • Helpful when CD45 is negative/dim and the differential includes small round blue cell tumor.
  • Highlights thymocytes in thymoma; usually absent in thymic carcinoma.
PatternNuclear staining.
NormalEarly hematogones, progenitor T cells and cortical thymocytes.
Positive diseaseB-ALL/LBL, T-ALL/LBL, mixed phenotype acute leukemia, BPDCN subset, thymoma-associated thymocytes; non-heme exceptions include Merkel cell carcinoma and germ cell tumors.
Usually negativeMature B-cell malignancies, Burkitt lymphoma, DLBCL, mantle cell lymphoma, most AML, Ewing sarcoma, thymic carcinoma.
Pearl: TdT-negative B-ALL can occur, particularly with KMT2A rearrangements; use full precursor panel and genetics.
