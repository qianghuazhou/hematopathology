T-Cell and NK-Cell Lymphoma Diagnostic Reference

Focused sign-out reference for suspected mature T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas: initial approach, core immunophenotyping, TFH vs PTCL-NOS, ALCL, extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, aggressive NK-cell leukemia, mycosis fungoides, clonality, pitfalls, and copy-ready report wording.

Practical frame

Do not diagnose T-cell lymphoma from one abnormal marker. Integrate architecture, cytology, distribution, immunophenotypic aberrancy, lineage/subset, EBV status, clonality, flow cytometry, and clinical context.

High-yield anchors

  • TFH lymphoma: CD4 plus ≥2–3 TFH markers and FDC/HEV pattern.
  • PTCL-NOS: diagnosis of exclusion after defined T/NK entities are excluded.
  • ENKTL: destructive extranodal lesion, cytotoxic phenotype, EBER-positive tumor cells.
  • ALCL: strong CD30 with appropriate morphology; then ALK, DUSP22/TP63 context.

Overview

Malignant T cells usually show atypical morphology, aberrant immunophenotype, and clonal T-cell receptor gene rearrangement or TRBC1 restriction. Aberrancy includes loss of pan-T markers such as CD5/CD7, homogeneous expansion of a T-cell subset, aberrant expression of non-native markers, or an abnormal flow phenotype. Clonality supports the diagnosis but is not by itself diagnostic.

Major Entities

Diagnosis of exclusion

PTCL, NOS

Nodal or extranodal mature T-cell lymphoma lacking defining features of TFH lymphoma, ALCL, ENKTL, or site-specific entities.

TFH lineage

Nodal TFH lymphoma

AI type, follicular type, or NOS. Requires TFH phenotype and compatible architecture.

CD30-driven

ALCL

Strong CD30, hallmark cells; classify ALK+ vs ALK− and consider site-specific entities.

EBV/cytotoxic

Extranodal NK/T

Angiocentric/angiodestructive cytotoxic lymphoma with EBER-positive tumor cells.

Skin

Mycosis fungoides

Epidermotropic cutaneous T-cell lymphoma; folliculotropic forms may show follicular mucinosis.

Systemic NK

Aggressive NK-cell leukemia

Systemic NK-cell neoplasm often with marrow/blood disease, cytotoxic phenotype, and hemophagocytosis.

TFH / AITL Approach

  1. Confirm mature T-cell proliferation, usually CD3+, CD4-predominant.
  2. Assess TFH markers: PD1, ICOS, CXCL13, CD10, BCL6; multiple markers are preferred.
  3. Look for HEV proliferation and expanded/disrupted CD21/CD23 follicular dendritic cell meshworks.
  4. Evaluate polymorphous background, plasma cells/eosinophils, and EBV-positive B-immunoblasts.
  5. Classify pattern: angioimmunoblastic type, follicular type, or NOS.
Essential AI-type pattern: nodal T-cell lymphoma, CD4+/CD8−, expression of multiple TFH markers, and extrafollicular FDC expansion around high endothelial venules.

PTCL-NOS Approach

PTCL-NOS is a mature T-cell lymphoma that remains after excluding specific entities. Typical features include diffuse or paracortical/T-zone expansion by atypical small-to-large pleomorphic T cells, aberrant loss of CD5 and/or CD7, variable CD4/CD8 phenotype, variable cytotoxic markers, and absence of a convincing TFH phenotype, ALCL morphology, or EBV-positive NK/T phenotype.

Helpful negative anchors: no diffuse TFH phenotype, no abnormal FDC expansion, ALK negative, no diagnostic ALCL morphology, EBER negative in tumor cells.

ALCL Approach

CD30 strong/diffuse large-cell lymphoma │ ├── ALK positive │ ↓ ALCL, ALK-positive │ ├── ALK negative │ ├── Nodal/systemic → ALCL, ALK-negative; consider DUSP22 and TP63 │ ├── Breast implant seroma/capsule → breast implant-associated ALCL │ └── Skin-limited → primary cutaneous ALCL │ └── Not classic ALCL morphology ↓ CD30+ PTCL, NOS / ENKTL / CHL mimic / transformed MF
Do not call ALCL on CD30 alone. Correlate with hallmark-cell morphology, T-cell/null phenotype, ALK, EBER, PAX5/CD15, and clinical site.

NK/T and Aggressive NK Approach

Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma

  • Extranodal destructive lesion, commonly nasal/oral/sinonasal but may occur elsewhere.
  • Angiocentricity/angiodestruction and necrosis are typical.
  • CD2+, cytoplasmic CD3ε+, CD56+, cytotoxic markers positive.
  • EBER ISH diffusely positive in tumor cells.
  • Usually negative for surface CD3, CD5, CD7, CD20/PAX5, ALK, keratins, SOX10, MPO.

Aggressive NK-cell leukemia

  • Systemic blood/marrow/splenic disease; may show intrasinusoidal or interstitial marrow infiltrates.
  • CD2+, CD56 bright, cytotoxic markers positive; CD5/CD7 often absent.
  • EBER may be positive, but EBV-negative cases exist.
  • Hemophagocytosis may be prominent.
  • Distinguish from chronic lymphoproliferative disorder of NK cells and reactive NK expansion.

Cutaneous T-cell Approach

  1. Confirm primary skin process vs secondary involvement by systemic T-cell lymphoma.
  2. MF clues: epidermotropism, Pautrier microabscesses, basal alignment of atypical lymphocytes, CD4 predominance, CD7/CD26 loss.
  3. Folliculotropic MF: follicular involvement, follicular mucinosis, less prominent epidermotropism in some cases.
  4. CD30+ lesions: distinguish lymphomatoid papulosis, primary cutaneous ALCL, transformed MF, and systemic ALCL involving skin.
  5. Use flow and TCR clonality as supportive evidence, not isolated proof.

Clonality and Flow Cytometry

TCR clonality by BIOMED-2 PCR and TRBC1 restriction by flow cytometry are supportive tools. A clonal T-cell result can occur in reactive or immune-driven processes, and false-negative/inconclusive results occur with poor DNA, low tumor burden, or sampling issues. Always interpret with morphology and phenotype.

Suggested wording:
T-cell receptor gene rearrangement studies show a clonal T-cell population / are negative / are pending. This result is interpreted in conjunction with the morphologic and immunophenotypic findings.

Key Pitfalls

  • Reactive T-cell proliferations may show partial CD7 loss and even clonal TCR peaks.
  • TFH markers are not specific alone; use a panel and architecture.
  • EBER-positive cells in AITL/nTFHL are often B-immunoblasts, not the neoplastic T cells.
  • ENKTL can be CD30-positive and mimic ALCL or Hodgkin lymphoma.
  • Limited core biopsies are often insufficient for definitive TFH lymphoma subclassification.
  • CD20/PAX5 should be checked in CD30-rich cases to exclude CHL or B-cell lymphoma mimics.
  • MF diagnosis is strengthened by clinicopathologic correlation and repeat biopsies when early lesions are subtle.
