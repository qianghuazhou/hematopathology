1. T-prolymphocytic leukemia

Reference diagnosis / setting

T-prolymphocytic leukemia; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Marked peripheral blood lymphocytosis with atypical medium-sized lymphocytes. Marrow markedly hypercellular and diffusely replaced by an atypical T-cell infiltrate, with markedly reduced hematopoiesis.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

CD2, CD3, CD4, CD5 and strong CD7 positive; CD8, TdT, CD34 and TCL1 negative in this example. Ki-67 approximately 50%. Flow identified a dominant aberrant mature T-cell population with unusual absent/dim CD45.

Interpretation and limitations

Peripheral blood and marrow findings support T-prolymphocytic leukemia. The TCL1-negative phenotype is an important limitation; genetic correlation is essential.

2. Aggressive NK-cell malignancy

Reference diagnosis / setting

Aggressive NK-cell malignancy; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Two reference patterns: (A) polymorphous nodular/interstitial infiltrates with abundant histiocytes and hemophagocytosis; (B) extensive interstitial and apparently intrasinusoidal infiltration by variably sized atypical lymphocytes, with granular cytoplasm in aspirate/blood.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

CD2 and CD56 positive with cytotoxic marker expression (TIA1, granzyme B and/or perforin); CD5, CD7 and TCR betaF1 negative. Cytoplasmic CD3 may be present. One reference example was EBER-negative and another EBER-positive.

Interpretation and limitations

Features favor aggressive NK-cell leukemia in the appropriate clinical setting. In the EBER-negative example, prior chronic NK-cell lymphoproliferation raised the possibility of progression; differential diagnosis requires clinical and genetic correlation.

3. Nodal TFH-cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type

Reference diagnosis / setting

Nodal TFH-cell lymphoma, angioimmunoblastic type; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Limited atypical interstitial lymphoid aggregates of small to medium T cells with irregular nuclei, admixed histiocytes and eosinophils. A separate follow-up example showed erythroid maturation arrest and megakaryocytic atypia.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

Atypical CD3/CD4-positive T cells express PD1 and ICOS, with BCL6 and/or CXCL13; CD7 may show partial loss. CD21 FDC meshworks may be absent. Aspirate flow was non-diagnostic in the examples.

Interpretation and limitations

Limited marrow involvement by previously diagnosed nodal TFH-cell lymphoma; a minute infiltrate may warrant 'suspicious for' rather than definitive wording. Associated cytopenias and dysplasia require assessment for therapy effect versus concurrent myeloid disease; clonal hematopoiesis can confound sequencing.

4. Aggressive large B-cell lymphoma, favor DLBCL

Reference diagnosis / setting

Aggressive large B-cell lymphoma, favor DLBCL; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Marrow extensively replaced by medium-to-large atypical B cells with marked fibrosis, crush artifact and focal osteosclerotic changes; residual hematopoiesis nearly absent. Aspirate markedly hemodilute and flow non-diagnostic.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

CD20, CD10, BCL6, BCL2 and MYC positive (MYC approximately 60%); MUM1, cyclin D1, TdT and EBER negative. Ki-67 approximately 60%.

Interpretation and limitations

Morphology and phenotype favor DLBCL with germinal-center phenotype. MYC/BCL2 protein expression raises concern but cannot establish rearranged disease; MYC/BCL2/BCL6 FISH and tissue correlation are appropriate.

5. Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma

Reference diagnosis / setting

Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Two examples: extensive diffuse small B-cell infiltrate with plasmacytoid forms, Dutcher bodies and mast cells; and limited residual disease manifested mainly as small aggregates of light-chain-restricted plasma cells without a distinct abnormal B-cell infiltrate.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

CD20-positive small B cells typically negative for CD5, CD10, BCL6 and cyclin D1. CD138-positive plasma cells show light-chain restriction. MYD88 L265P detected in the extensive-involvement example; follow-up flow was non-diagnostic in the plasma-cell-predominant example.

Interpretation and limitations

Interpret with known LPL history, paraprotein and MYD88 status. Persistent plasmacytic disease may be present despite no detectable clonal B-cell population on aspirate flow.

6. B-cell lymphoma/leukemia, favor splenic or marginal zone lymphoma

Reference diagnosis / setting

B-cell lymphoma/leukemia, favor splenic or marginal zone lymphoma; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Marked circulating atypical small-to-medium lymphocytes, some with irregular or flower-like nuclei, and extensive diffuse marrow infiltration without sheets of large cells.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

Clonal CD19/CD20-bright B cells lacking CD5, CD10, CD23 and CD200 in the example. PAX5, BCL2 and IgD positive; cyclin D1, SOX11, BCL6 and EBER negative. Ki-67 approximately 20%.

Interpretation and limitations

Mature B-cell lymphoma/leukemia favoring splenic/marginal zone category, not definitively subtyped from marrow alone. Correlate with splenic imaging, peripheral blood and other tissue or molecular studies.

7. Plasma cell neoplasm

Reference diagnosis / setting

Plasma cell neoplasm; educational summary of source marrow findings.

Peripheral blood and marrow morphology

Normocellular marrow with interstitial atypical plasma cells involving approximately 20% of cellularity by core immunostain; aspirate differential yielded a lower estimate. Background trilineage hematopoiesis preserved.

Immunophenotype and ancillary studies

CD138-positive kappa-restricted plasma cells, partial CD117 positive, CD56 and cyclin D1 negative; Ki-67 approximately 30%. A small clonal plasma-cell population was identified on aspirate flow.

Interpretation and limitations

Marrow involvement by a plasma cell neoplasm. Classification and disease status require clinical, biochemical, imaging and cytogenetic correlation.
