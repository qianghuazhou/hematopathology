CSTM 2026 Report Back

Practical Implications for the Transfusion Medicine Committee

Steven Zhou, Medical Director Transfusion Medicine Committee June 7, 2026

Executive Summary

The most actionable themes from CSTM 2026 were transfusion stewardship, objective documentation of transfusion medicine medical-director workload, competency documentation, data-driven guideline implementation, and prevention of red-cell alloimmunization. Several topics could be translated into local quality improvement projects or committee work within the next year.

1. Medical Director Workload Documentation and Business Case Development

Immediate QI / resource planning

Key message

  • A structured workload document can quantify transfusion medicine medical-director activities that are often invisible.
  • Track consultation, utilization review, policy work, QA, accreditation, education, committee work, incident review, and urgent blood-bank support.
  • Objective workload data can support business cases for protected medical-director time, administrative support, and laboratory resources.

Potential local action

  • Create a simple local workload tracker for 3–6 months.
  • Separate routine work from urgent/unplanned work.
  • Use data to support future discussion of protected medical-director time and service expansion.

2. Bloody Easy Lite eLearning for Competency Assessment

Regulatory / accreditation relevance

Key message

  • Bloody Easy Lite can support standardized transfusion medicine education and competency assessment.
  • Electronic tracking may simplify documentation for audits and accreditation.
  • The tool can help identify staff knowledge gaps and guide ongoing education.

Potential local action

  • Compare current competency documentation with regulatory and accreditation expectations.
  • Consider pilot use for new staff, annual competency, or focused retraining after incidents.
  • Use completion reports as part of quality documentation.

3. Albumin and Platelet Guideline Implementation

Stewardship / informatics

Key message

  • Guideline implementation requires more than education: it needs utilization monitoring, clinician engagement, feedback, and repeat audit.
  • Data dashboards and informatics can identify outlier ordering patterns and opportunities for stewardship.
  • Albumin and platelet use are practical targets for local transfusion stewardship.

Potential local action

  • Review albumin orders by indication, service, dose, and repeat use.
  • Review platelet transfusions for threshold, indication, bleeding status, and procedural use.
  • Develop a small dashboard for TM Committee review every 3–6 months.

4. c- and E-Matched RBCs for Women of Child-Bearing Potential

Alloimmunization prevention

Key message

  • Anti-c and anti-E can cause hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn.
  • A Mount Sinai Hospital pilot implemented Rh/K matching for females <45 years and maintained c−E−K− inventory.
  • In the reported pilot, 494 patients and 760 crossmatch requests were evaluated; 83% underwent Rh/K phenotyping, 95.3% received matched units, and 4.7% received unmatched units because of time or inventory limitations.
  • Mean phenotyping turnaround time was reported as 15 minutes.

Potential local action

  • Estimate local transfusion volume for females of child-bearing potential.
  • Review local inventory feasibility for c−E−K− or phenotype-matched units.
  • Discuss whether a targeted policy is feasible for non-urgent transfusions.
  • Track exceptions: urgent release, phenotype unavailable, and inventory limitation.

5. IgG Utilization Management

Demand growth / stewardship

Key message

  • IgG demand continues to grow in Canada, especially for secondary immunodeficiency.
  • A national shortage-management framework and Ontario-wide IT system are being developed to support standardized adjudication and reporting.
  • Distribution modernization, SCIG pathways, and pharmacist integration may improve stewardship and reduce cost.

Potential local action

  • Review local IgG request, approval, and renewal workflow.
  • Identify high-volume indications and repeat users.
  • Align local process with provincial adjudication and reporting systems as they become available.

6. Transfusion Stewardship and Reducing Low-Value Obstetrical Testing

Practice review

Key message

  • Routine maternal group-and-screen testing at delivery may not be required in many pregnancies if appropriate antenatal testing is already completed.
  • Routine cord blood testing should be targeted to specific clinical indications rather than performed universally.
  • RhIG use in very early pregnancy loss is being re-evaluated, with movement toward more selective use.
  • Antenatal FMH testing appears to have limited value in uncomplicated Rh-negative pregnancies, particularly before 25 weeks.

Potential local action

  • Map current obstetrical testing patterns.
  • Review cord blood testing indications with obstetrics and laboratory leadership.
  • Assess whether local FMH testing practices match current evidence and provincial guidance.

7. Sickle Cell Disease: HSCT and Gene Therapy Support

Specialized referral pathway

Key message

  • Curative therapies for sickle cell disease create unique transfusion support challenges.
  • Extended antigen matching, prolonged transfusion support, and alloimmunization prevention are central issues.
  • Blood bank communication with transplant/gene therapy centres is essential.

Potential local action

  • Ensure patients referred for curative therapy have clear transfusion history and antibody documentation.
  • Confirm availability of extended phenotype/genotype information.
  • Coordinate early with referral centres if transfusion support is expected locally.

8. Cold-Stored Platelets in Cardiac Surgery

Emerging evidence

Key message

  • Cold-stored platelets may have improved hemostatic function for actively bleeding patients.
  • Canadian trials are evaluating efficacy, safety, and operational feasibility compared with room-temperature platelets.
  • This remains investigational and may affect future platelet inventory strategy.

Potential local action

  • Monitor trial results and future guideline recommendations.
  • Consider potential relevance to cardiac surgery, trauma, and massive hemorrhage protocols if evidence supports adoption.
