Acute Hemolytic Reaction

Reaction

Suspected acute transfusion reaction during or shortly after transfusion.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Evaluation of suspected acute hemolytic transfusion reaction.

Clinical information:
Patient developed [fever / hypotension / hemoglobinuria / back or flank pain / other] during or shortly after transfusion of [component]. Clerical check: [___]. DAT: [___]. Repeat ABO/Rh: [___]. Hemolysis markers: [___].

Assessment:
The clinical and laboratory findings are [consistent / not consistent] with an acute hemolytic transfusion reaction. [Brief rationale, including relevant DAT, hemolysis, serologic, or clerical findings.]

Recommendations:
• Stop the implicated transfusion and maintain IV access with normal saline if the transfusion is still in progress.
• Monitor CBC, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin, renal function, urine output, and clinical status as indicated.
• If further transfusion is required, provide appropriately compatible RBCs based on the serologic findings.
• Complete transfusion-reaction investigation and hemovigilance reporting as applicable.

Delayed Hemolytic Transfusion Reaction

Reaction

Post-transfusion hemoglobin decline with new alloantibody and hemolysis.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Evaluation of suspected delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction.

Clinical information:
Patient received RBC transfusion on [date] and subsequently developed [falling hemoglobin / jaundice / biochemical hemolysis]. DAT: [___]. Antibody screen/identification: [___]. Relevant transfusion history: [___].

Assessment:
Findings are [consistent / not consistent] with a delayed hemolytic transfusion reaction associated with [antibody]. The reaction reflects an anamnestic alloantibody response with accelerated clearance of transfused antigen-positive RBCs.

Recommendations:
• Provide [antigen]-negative, crossmatch-compatible RBCs for future transfusions.
• Maintain the antibody permanently in the transfusion-service history even if it later becomes undetectable.
• Monitor hemoglobin and hemolysis parameters until clinically stable.
• Transfuse further RBCs only as clinically indicated.

Compatibility

Management of patients with multiple RBC alloantibodies and difficult compatibility.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Transfusion support in a patient with complex RBC alloantibodies.

Clinical information:
Patient has a history of [transfusions / pregnancy / prior alloantibodies]. Current antibodies: [___]. RBC phenotype/genotype: [___]. Current transfusion requirement: [___].

Assessment:
The presence of [antibodies] limits the compatible donor pool and may delay provision of RBCs. Clinically significant historical antibodies must continue to be honored even if not currently detectable.

Recommendations:

Clinical information:

Assessment:
The pattern is most consistent with [immune / non-immune] platelet refractoriness. Non-immune clinical factors are the most common cause; immune refractoriness is supported by poor early corrected count increments and demonstrable platelet-reactive/HLA antibodies.

Recommendations:

• Address contributing non-immune factors where possible.
• If immune refractoriness is confirmed, use [HLA-selected / crossmatch-compatible] platelets according to availability and response.
• Continue to document post-transfusion increments to assess effectiveness of selected products.

Massive Hemorrhage Protocol

Urgent Support

Guidance for balanced component therapy in severe hemorrhage.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Massive hemorrhage / massive transfusion management.

Clinical information:
Patient with active major bleeding due to [trauma / obstetric hemorrhage / surgery / GI bleeding / other]. Products transfused to date: [___]. Hemoglobin: [___]. Platelets: [___]. Fibrinogen: [___]. INR/aPTT: [___]. Ionized calcium: [___].

Assessment:
Ongoing major hemorrhage requires rapid balanced component support with frequent reassessment. Product therapy should be adjusted to bleeding, laboratory results, and the clinical response rather than continued empirically once hemorrhage is controlled.

Recommendations:
• Continue the institutional massive hemorrhage protocol while major bleeding persists.
• Provide RBCs and plasma according to the institutional MHP ratio, with platelets and fibrinogen replacement as indicated.
• Monitor CBC, INR/aPTT, fibrinogen, ionized calcium, temperature, and acid-base status.
• Replace calcium and fibrinogen as required.
• Deactivate the MHP promptly once bleeding is controlled and transition to goal-directed transfusion.

Obstetric ITP – Delivery Planning

ITP

Peripartum platelet targets and treatment planning for maternal immune thrombocytopenia.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Peripartum management of immune thrombocytopenia (ITP).

Clinical information:
Gestational age: [___] weeks. Platelet count: [___] × 10⁹/L. Planned delivery: [vaginal / induction / cesarean / not yet determined]. Bleeding symptoms: [___]. Current ITP therapy: [___].

Assessment:
Management is guided by bleeding risk and the platelet count required for delivery and anesthesia. A platelet count ≥70 × 10⁹/L is generally considered reasonable for neuraxial anesthesia when stable and without additional coagulopathy; ≥50 × 10⁹/L is targeted for cesarean delivery, while approximately ≥30 × 10⁹/L is generally adequate for vaginal delivery itself. ITP alone is not an indication for cesarean delivery.

Recommendations:
• If a rapid platelet rise is required near delivery, use IVIG; corticosteroids may be added or used when sufficient time is available.
• Recheck the platelet count close to delivery and before neuraxial anesthesia.
• Platelet transfusion is not routinely indicated in ITP without bleeding; for urgent cesarean with an inadequate count, transfuse peri-operatively to achieve >50 × 10⁹/L, preferably with concurrent ITP therapy.
• Avoid fetal scalp electrodes and operative vaginal instrumentation when possible.
• Monitor the maternal platelet count postpartum.

IVIG Vital Sign Monitoring

IVIG

Risk-stratified vital-sign monitoring during IVIG infusion.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
IVIG vital-sign monitoring.

Clinical information:
Patient receiving [IVIG product/dose] and has tolerated [number] previous infusions without significant reaction. Relevant risk factors: [___].

Assessment:
IVIG infusion reactions are usually mild and rate-related, occur predominantly early in the infusion, and decrease with repeated tolerance. Published experience suggests a low yield from frequent routine vital-sign measurements in stable patients receiving established IVIG therapy. Product labeling requires clinical monitoring throughout infusion but does not mandate a specific vital-sign interval.

Recommendations:
• For patients who have repeatedly tolerated the same IVIG product, obtain vital signs at baseline, with significant rate escalation, and at completion of infusion.
• Obtain additional vital signs promptly if symptoms or clinical concerns develop.
• Maintain more frequent monitoring for first infusions, product changes, previous infusion reactions, or clinically high-risk patients.
• Slow or stop the infusion and reassess if a suspected reaction occurs.

Albumin Use / Fluid Drainage

Albumin

Assessment of albumin replacement after paracentesis and/or thoracentesis.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Albumin use following [paracentesis / thoracentesis / combined fluid drainage].

Clinical information:
Ascitic fluid removed: [___] L. Pleural fluid removed: [___] L. Relevant factors: [cirrhosis / acute-on-chronic liver failure / hypotension / renal dysfunction / electrolyte abnormality / other].

Assessment:
For large-volume paracentesis in cirrhosis (>5 L), 20–25% albumin at approximately 6–8 g/L of ascitic fluid removed is recommended to reduce post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction. Routine volume-based albumin replacement after thoracentesis, including hepatic hydrothorax, is not supported by established guideline evidence; pleural fluid volume should therefore not routinely be added to the paracentesis calculation.

Recommendations:
• Albumin dose for paracentesis: [___] L × 6–8 g/L = [___–___] g.
• 25% albumin contains 25 g/100 mL; calculated volume = [___] mL.
• Consider albumin after smaller-volume paracentesis when clinically indicated by hypotension, renal dysfunction, electrolyte abnormalities, or acute-on-chronic liver failure.
• Do not routinely replace thoracentesis volume with albumin.
• Monitor hemodynamics, renal function, electrolytes, and volume status.

Parvovirus B19-Positive RBC Unit

Infectious Risk

Recipient assessment and follow-up after transfusion of an RBC unit subsequently reported as Parvovirus B19 positive.

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Recipient of an RBC unit subsequently reported as Parvovirus B19 (B19V) positive.

Clinical information:
Patient received [component/unit] on [date]. Relevant risk factors: [immunocompromised / hematologic malignancy or transplant / chronic hemolytic disorder / pregnancy / none identified]. Donor B19V viral load/IgG status: [___].

Assessment:
Transfusion-transmitted B19V infection is uncommon. Risk is driven primarily by donor viral load and antibody status and by recipient susceptibility. Clinically significant infection is of greatest concern in immunocompromised patients, patients with increased erythrocyte turnover, and susceptible pregnant patients.

Recommendations:
• Confirm donor quantitative B19V viral load and, if available, anti-B19V IgG status.
• Review baseline CBC/hemoglobin and reticulocyte count and monitor clinically over the following weeks.
• If unexplained anemia with reticulocytopenia develops, obtain quantitative B19V PCR; PCR is preferred in immunocompromised patients.
• If clinically significant persistent infection is confirmed, involve the appropriate clinical service and consider IVIG where indicated.
• Trace/quarantine untransfused co-components and complete hemovigilance actions as applicable.

Cellular component safety

Non-Irradiated Component Exposure / TA-GVHD Risk

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Inadvertent transfusion of a non-irradiated cellular blood component in a patient with an indication for irradiated products.

Clinical information:
Underlying condition/irradiation indication: [___]. Patient received [___] non-irradiated [RBC / platelet] unit(s) on [date(s)]. Component age, if available: [___]. Current clinical status: [___].

Assessment:
The patient has had an inadvertent exposure to viable donor lymphocytes and therefore a potential risk of transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease (TA-GVHD). The absolute risk after an individual exposure is uncertain and depends on recipient susceptibility and donor-recipient HLA relationships. Leukoreduction does not replace irradiation for prevention of TA-GVHD. There is no established post-exposure intervention that reliably prevents TA-GVHD once a non-irradiated component has been transfused.

Recommendations:
• Ensure a permanent requirement/flag for irradiated cellular blood components for future transfusions, according to the underlying indication.
• No prophylactic therapy is routinely recommended solely because of the exposure.
• Monitor clinically during the subsequent 2–6 weeks for fever, rash, diarrhea, hepatitis/liver dysfunction, and otherwise unexplained cytopenias.
• Obtain CBC and liver enzymes if symptoms develop; scheduled surveillance may be considered in a high-risk exposure.
• If TA-GVHD is clinically suspected, urgently involve Hematology/Transfusion Medicine and pursue appropriate diagnostic testing.
• Document the event and complete institutional/hemovigilance review as applicable.
RBC serology

Warm Autoantibody / AIHA Transfusion Support

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
RBC transfusion support in a patient with a warm autoantibody / autoimmune hemolytic anemia.

Clinical information:
Hemoglobin: [___] g/L. Evidence of hemolysis: [___]. DAT: [___]. Antibody screen/panel: [___]. Historical alloantibodies: [___]. Previous transfusions/pregnancies: [___]. Current transfusion requirement: [___].

Assessment:
Serologic incompatibility is expected in the presence of a warm autoantibody and does not by itself preclude RBC transfusion when clinically indicated. The key transfusion-service objective is to identify or exclude clinically significant underlying alloantibodies and provide antigen-negative RBCs when required. The decision to transfuse should be based on the patient's clinical status and severity of anemia rather than compatibility-test strength alone.

Recommendations:
• Complete appropriate serologic evaluation to identify/exclude underlying alloantibodies; adsorption and/or RBC genotyping may be required.
• Honor all current and historical clinically significant alloantibodies with antigen-negative RBCs.
• If transfusion is clinically necessary, do not delay solely because crossmatch-compatible units cannot be obtained due to the warm autoantibody.
• Provide the safest appropriately selected RBC units available and transfuse according to clinical need.
• Monitor hemoglobin and clinical/biochemical evidence of hemolysis after transfusion as indicated.
• For urgent transfusion before serologic investigation is complete, contact Transfusion Medicine to determine the safest available RBC selection.
Emergency transfusion

Emergency / Incompatible RBC Release

TRANSFUSION MEDICINE CONSULTATION

Reason for consultation:
Emergency RBC transfusion when fully compatible blood is not immediately available.

Clinical information:
Patient has [active life-threatening bleeding / severe symptomatic anemia] requiring urgent transfusion. ABO/Rh: [known / pending]. Current/historical antibodies: [___]. Compatibility issue: [___]. Available RBC options: [___].

Assessment:
The immediate risk of delaying transfusion is judged to exceed the potential risk associated with transfusing the safest available incompletely tested or serologically incompatible RBCs. Product selection should prioritize ABO compatibility and avoidance of known clinically significant antigens whenever feasible without causing a clinically important delay.

Recommendations:
• Do not delay life-saving transfusion while complete compatibility testing is pending.
• Provide emergency-release RBCs according to institutional policy and the patient's known ABO/Rh type when established.
• Honor known clinically significant alloantibodies whenever antigen-negative units can be provided without unacceptable delay.
• If fully compatible units are unavailable, issue the safest available RBCs following Transfusion Medicine risk assessment and document the rationale.
• Continue antibody investigation/crossmatching concurrently and transition to antigen-negative, crossmatch-compatible units as soon as feasible.
• Monitor for clinical and laboratory evidence of hemolysis when incompatible or incompletely tested RBCs have been transfused.
• Document emergency release/authorization and communicate the transfusion plan to the treating team.
