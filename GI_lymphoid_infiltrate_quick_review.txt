GI LYMPHOID INFILTRATES — QUICK INTERNAL HEMATOPATHOLOGY REVIEW

REFERRAL REQUIREMENTS
For GI biopsies referred for lymphoid aggregates/infiltrates, obtain CD3 and CD20 as the minimum initial stains. Include H&E, relevant clinical/endoscopic history, biopsy site, and any prior lymphoma diagnosis. Additional studies are directed by morphology and hematopathology review.

MORPHOLOGY — FEATURES OF CONCERN
- Dense, confluent, expansile, or deeply infiltrative lymphoid infiltrate rather than a discrete aggregate.
- Monomorphic lymphocytes or cytologic atypia (irregular nuclei, conspicuous nucleoli, increased large cells).
- Destruction or displacement of glands, convincing lymphoepithelial lesions, or architectural effacement.
- Abnormal follicles (back-to-back follicles, absent polarization, attenuated mantle zones, or atypical follicular cytology).
- Monocytoid B-cell appearance, plasmacytic differentiation with atypia, or Dutcher bodies.
- Increased mitoses, apoptosis, necrosis, or a conspicuous high-grade component.
- Discordance with the expected inflammatory pattern, a mass lesion, or known lymphoma.

No single feature or numerical count establishes lymphoma. Evaluate morphology, CD3/CD20 distribution, biopsy site, and clinical history together. Lymphoepithelial lesions must be distinguished from reactive epithelial infiltration. Small or crushed samples may limit assessment.

MORPHOLOGY — ADDITIONAL DESCRIPTIVE PHRASES
- Small, well-circumscribed lymphoid aggregate in the lamina propria, composed of polymorphous small lymphocytes without glandular distortion.
- Prominent lymphoid follicle with a polarized germinal center, tingible-body macrophages, and a well-defined mantle zone.
- Patchy lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate within the lamina propria, with preserved crypt/gland architecture.
- Multiple closely spaced lymphoid aggregates with focal confluence but without overt epithelial destruction.
- Dense, monotonous small lymphoid infiltrate expanding the lamina propria and separating native glands.
- Nodular or diffuse infiltrate extending into the muscularis mucosae or submucosa where sampled.
- Small lymphocytes with irregular nuclear contours and scant cytoplasm, with focal monocytoid appearance.
- Atypical lymphoid cells infiltrating and disrupting glandular epithelium, raising concern for lymphoepithelial lesions.
- Follicles lacking polarization or mantle zones, with atypical germinal-center cells or follicular colonization.
- Scattered large atypical lymphoid cells with vesicular chromatin and prominent nucleoli in a background of small lymphocytes.
- Plasma-cell-rich infiltrate with possible cytologic atypia; consider light-chain studies if a clonal process is suspected.
- Crush artifact, superficial sampling, or scant lymphoid tissue limits assessment of architecture and cytology.

These are selectable morphologic descriptions, not diagnostic criteria; adapt to the observed tissue and avoid implying lymphoma from density or CD20 predominance alone.

QUICK REVIEW COMMENTS

1. REACTIVE LYMPHOID AGGREGATE
The lymphoid aggregate is polymorphous, with admixed CD3-positive T cells and CD20-positive B cells. No significant cytologic atypia or destructive growth. Favor reactive lymphoid aggregate.

2. REACTIVE LYMPHOID FOLLICLE
The lymphoid follicle shows preserved architecture, with CD20-positive B cells and surrounding CD3-positive T cells. No concerning morphologic features. Favor reactive lymphoid follicle.

3. NONSPECIFIC LYMPHOID INFILTRATE
Focal mixed T- and B-cell infiltrate without significant cytologic atypia or destructive features. No convincing morphologic evidence of lymphoma.

4. T-CELL-PREDOMINANT INFILTRATE
Predominantly CD3-positive T-cell infiltrate with scattered CD20-positive B cells. No overt atypia or destructive features. Favor reactive/inflammatory infiltrate.

5. B-CELL-PREDOMINANT AGGREGATE
CD20-positive B-cell-predominant aggregate with admixed CD3-positive T cells. No significant atypia or destructive features. Favor reactive lymphoid aggregate.

6. LIMITED LYMPHOID AGGREGATE
Small/limited lymphoid aggregate without overt atypia on available sections. CD3 and CD20 demonstrate T and B cells. Definitive characterization is limited by sample size.

7. ATYPICAL B-CELL INFILTRATE
Atypical CD20-positive B-cell-predominant infiltrate with [specify concerning features]. A B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder cannot be excluded. Additional immunohistochemical studies are recommended.

8. ATYPICAL T-CELL INFILTRATE
Atypical CD3-positive T-cell-predominant infiltrate with [specify concerning features]. Further immunophenotypic characterization is recommended.

9. SUSPICIOUS FOR LYMPHOMA
The infiltrate shows [specify concerning features], suspicious for lymphoma. Further hematopathology workup is required for classification.

10. POSSIBLE GASTRIC MALT LYMPHOMA
Dense B-cell-predominant infiltrate with [specify concerning features, e.g., destructive lymphoepithelial lesions/monocytoid cytology]. Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of MALT type cannot be excluded. Recommend targeted immunohistochemical studies and assessment of H. pylori status.

11. PRIOR LYMPHOMA
Lymphoid infiltrate identified. Given the history of [lymphoma type], comparison with prior material and targeted immunohistochemical studies are recommended to assess involvement.

12. NO EVIDENCE OF INVOLVEMENT BY KNOWN LYMPHOMA
The infiltrate shows no convincing morphologic or CD3/CD20 immunophenotypic evidence of involvement by the patient's known [lymphoma type] in this limited biopsy. Additional targeted studies may be needed if clinical suspicion persists.

ESCALATION
- Reactive morphology: brief comment; no routine additional IHC.
- Indeterminate morphology: select targeted IHC after hematopathology review, preserving tissue.
- Suspicious/destructive infiltrate, high-grade cytology, or significant discordance with history: formal hematopathology workup; consider repeat biopsy if insufficient tissue.

Note: CD3/CD20 distribution alone cannot establish clonality or exclude lymphoma. Comments should be adapted to actual morphology and stains reviewed.
