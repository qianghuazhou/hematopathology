MINT Trial

Restrictive or Liberal Transfusion Strategy in Myocardial Infarction and Anemia

Carson et al. New England Journal of Medicine. 2023. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2307983

Clinical Question

In patients with myocardial infarction and anemia, does a restrictive or liberal transfusion strategy reduce myocardial ischemia or death at 30 days?

Background

  • Anemia is common in acute myocardial infarction and may worsen ischemic injury by reducing oxygen delivery to vulnerable myocardium.
  • The optimal hemoglobin target is uncertain because clinicians must balance myocardial oxygen delivery against transfusion-related risks, including transfusion-associated circulatory overload.
  • Earlier randomized trials in MI had smaller sample sizes and produced inconsistent findings.
  • Most prior ICU and perioperative trials favored restrictive transfusion thresholds, but those data may not directly apply to acute coronary ischemia.
MINT was the largest randomized trial to date addressing transfusion thresholds in acute myocardial infarction with anemia.

Study Design

  • Open-label, multicenter randomized controlled trial
  • 1:1 allocation by web-based randomization
  • Permuted block design with random block sizes of 4 to 6
  • Stratified by clinical site
  • Prespecified subgroup analyses included MI type, revascularization, heart failure, hemoglobin, acute versus chronic anemia, renal function, diabetes, age, and race
  • Blinded central adjudication of outcomes

Setting

  • 144 sites across the United States, Canada, France, Brazil, New Zealand, and Australia
  • Recruitment from April 2017 through April 2023

Population

Inclusion Criteria

  • Age 18 years or older
  • STEMI or NSTEMI during the index hospitalization
  • At least one troponin above the upper limit of normal plus clinical, ECG, imaging, or angiographic evidence of MI
  • Hemoglobin concentration <100 g/L at randomization

MI Definitions Included

  • Type 1 MI: spontaneous MI related to coronary arterial disease
  • Type 2 MI: secondary ischemic imbalance
  • Type 4b MI: stent thrombosis
  • Type 4c MI: severe in-stent restenosis without thrombosis

Exclusion Criteria

  • Uncontrolled acute bleeding requiring uncrossmatched or non-type-specific blood
  • Declined transfusion
  • Planned cardiac surgery during the admission
  • Palliative treatment only
  • Known inability to complete 30-day follow-up
  • Prior enrollment in MINT
  • Enrollment in a competing clinical study

Sample Size and Baseline Characteristics

  • Planned enrollment: 3500 patients
  • Enrolled: 3506
  • Primary analysis: 3504 patients
  • Mean age: 72 years in both groups
  • Female: 44% vs 47%
  • About one-third of patients had already received packed red cells before randomization
  • Groups were otherwise well matched for MI type, interventions, and heart failure
  • Most patients had Type 2 MI (55.8%); 41.7% had Type 1 MI

Intervention

Restrictive strategy

  • Transfusion permitted but not required when hemoglobin was <80 g/L
  • Transfusion strongly recommended when hemoglobin was <70 g/L or when anginal symptoms were not controlled with medication
  • Mean hemoglobin on day 3: 8.9 g/dL
  • Protocol discontinuation: 2.6%

Control

Liberal strategy

  • 1 unit of packed red cells after randomization, then additional transfusion as needed when hemoglobin was <100 g/L
  • Continued until hospital discharge or day 30
  • Mean hemoglobin on day 3: 10.5 g/dL
  • Protocol discontinuation: 13.7%

Management Common to Both Groups

  • Red cells were transfused one unit at a time followed by repeat hemoglobin measurement
  • Protocol could be paused if active bleeding requiring transfusion occurred
  • Transfusion could be delayed in volume-overloaded patients until diuresis or dialysis

Primary Outcome

Composite of myocardial infarction or death from any cause at 30 days

Restrictive: 16.9%
Liberal: 14.5%

Risk ratio 1.15 with multiple imputation for incomplete follow-up

The primary endpoint numerically favored the liberal strategy, but the trial did not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance.

Transfusion Exposure

  • Total red cell use was about 3.5 times higher in the liberal group
  • Mean units per patient:
    • Restrictive: 0.7 ± 1.6
    • Liberal: 2.5 ± 2.3

Secondary Outcomes

Comparing restrictive versus liberal strategy:

  • Cardiac death: 5.5% vs 3.2%
  • Death from any cause: 9.9% vs 8.3%
  • Myocardial infarction: 8.5% vs 7.2%
  • Unscheduled revascularization: 2.5% vs 2.2%
  • Stroke: 1.7% vs 1.5%
  • Pneumonia or bacteremia: 9.5% vs 8.7%
  • Heart failure: 5.8% vs 6.3%
  • PE or DVT: 1.5% vs 1.9%
Most clinically important outcomes numerically favored the liberal strategy, while heart failure and venous thromboembolism did not appear increased.

Subgroup Analysis

Prespecified subgroups suggesting greater benefit with the liberal strategy included:

  • Type 1 MI
  • Congestive heart failure, acute heart failure, or reduced LVEF

Other subgroups generally also numerically favored the liberal strategy, though most did not show a statistically significant interaction.

Interpretation and Statistical Context

  • The primary endpoint narrowly missed conventional significance.
  • The confidence interval did not suggest a clear benefit for a restrictive strategy and remained compatible with clinically important benefit for the liberal strategy.
  • Secondary and subgroup signals were directionally consistent with possible harm from overly restrictive transfusion in acute coronary ischemia.

Authors’ Conclusions

In patients with acute myocardial infarction and anemia, a liberal transfusion strategy did not significantly reduce recurrent MI or death at 30 days, but the pattern of results suggested possible clinical benefit that would need confirmation in further studies.

Strengths

  • Large pragmatic multicenter randomized trial
  • Much larger than prior MI transfusion trials
  • Broad inclusion criteria improve external validity
  • Meaningful separation in hemoglobin levels and transfusion exposure between groups
  • Excellent 30-day follow-up
  • Blinded central adjudication of outcomes

Weaknesses

  • Open-label intervention
  • Only MI in the primary outcome was centrally adjudicated
  • Imperfect adherence to the liberal strategy may have biased toward no difference
  • No reported screened-but-not-enrolled denominator in the main summary here
  • No adjustment for multiplicity of multiple secondary outcomes
  • Some subgroup findings are mechanistically difficult to explain and should be interpreted cautiously

Contemporary Guideline Context

  • The 2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI ACS guideline states that transfusion to maintain hemoglobin at 10 g/dL may be reasonable in ACS patients with acute or chronic anemia who are not actively bleeding.
  • The 2025 AABB AMI guideline suggests a liberal red cell transfusion strategy when hemoglobin is less than 10 g/dL in hospitalized patients with AMI.
MINT did not produce a formally positive primary trial result, but it was influential enough to shift modern ACS guidance toward considering a hemoglobin target around 10 g/dL in AMI with anemia.

Bottom Line

In myocardial infarction with anemia, a liberal transfusion strategy targeting about 100 g/L (10 g/dL) appears at least reasonable and may be preferable to a restrictive strategy, especially in higher-risk ACS phenotypes such as Type 1 MI or heart failure.

Transfusion decisions should still be individualized, with attention to volume status, risk of transfusion-associated circulatory overload, ongoing ischemia, and overall clinical context.
