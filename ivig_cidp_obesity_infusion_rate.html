IVIG Maintenance in Severe Obesity: Dose and Infusion Rate

Clinical reasoning, practical rate conversion, and a planning calculator

Reviewed November 2026 · Educational reference

De-identified clinical scenario

An adult with MADSAM (a CIDP variant) and severe obesity (BMI >50; actual weight approximately 180–190 kg) had objective motor improvement following three courses of IVIG, approximately 2 g/kg per course based on adjusted weight, every three weeks. Each course was administered over three days with a maximum rate of 200 mL/h. Delayed fatigue was substantial despite no major immediate infusion reaction.

The neurologist proposed reducing maintenance therapy to approximately 1 g/kg adjusted weight every four weeks and administering the new approximately 120 g dose in one day, with a proposed maximum rate of 250–300 mL/h.

Transfusion medicine assessment: Cautious escalation to 250 mL/h may be considered after individual thrombotic, renal, cardiovascular, hydration and volume-status review, using the institution's titration protocol. This is a proposed plan, not a documented post-escalation outcome.

Key distinctions

  • Dose: Gamunex-C CIDP labeling describes 1 g/kg every three weeks; a four-week interval is individualized based on response and tolerability.
  • Weight scalar: Adjusted-weight dosing may be used under local policy; clinical trial dosing and product-label dosing should not be assumed to validate adjusted-weight equivalence.
  • Single-day treatment: Gamunex-C labeling permits the CIDP maintenance dose in one day or divided over two days. Single-day feasibility depends on infusion hours and monitoring.
  • Rate: A product-label maximum is a ceiling, not a goal. For thrombosis or renal risk, use the minimum practicable rate.
  • Delayed fatigue: Previous tolerance during infusion does not exclude clinically important delayed effects.

Pre-infusion review

  • Prior VTE/arterial thrombosis, mobility limitations, vascular risk factors, hyperviscosity concerns
  • Creatinine/eGFR, hydration and volume status, diabetes, relevant medications
  • Cardiac dysfunction, fluid intolerance, blood pressure
  • Previous immediate and delayed reactions; reassess fatigue after rate change
  • Consider hemoglobin/hemolysis assessment according to dose, blood group, and symptoms

Split dosing (e.g., 60 g on two days) remains an option if clinically indicated; a lower daily burden does not by itself establish a quantified reduction in thrombotic risk.

Practice takeaway

In a patient with objective response to IVIG but substantial treatment burden, dose/interval reduction and a cautious single-day infusion strategy can be considered separately. Escalation from 200 to 250 mL/h should depend on individual risk assessment and local protocol, not merely on the manufacturer's maximum permitted rate or experience in other patients.
