Blood Products

Overview of commonly used blood components in transfusion medicine, including their composition and major clinical indications.

Red Blood Cells (RBCs) Cellular Component

Packed red blood cells are the most frequently transfused blood product and are used to increase oxygen-carrying capacity.

Common Indications
  • Symptomatic anemia
  • Acute blood loss
  • Perioperative anemia
  • Chronic transfusion therapy (e.g., sickle cell disease, thalassemia)
Typical Dose
  • Adults: 1 unit increases hemoglobin by ~10 g/L
  • Infants/children: 10–15 mL/kg increases Hb by ~20–30 g/L
Special Modifications
  • Leukoreduced
  • Irradiated
  • Washed
  • CMV-negative
  • Frozen/deglycerolized

Platelets Cellular Component

Platelet transfusion is used to prevent or treat bleeding due to thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction.

Common Prophylactic Thresholds
  • <10 ×10⁹/L – stable patients
  • <20 ×10⁹/L – fever, infection, coagulopathy
  • <50 ×10⁹/L – invasive procedures or active bleeding
  • <100 ×10⁹/L – neurosurgery or head trauma
Product Types
  • Apheresis platelets (single donor)
  • Pooled platelet concentrates
Special Situations
  • Washed platelets for IgA deficiency with anti-IgA
  • HLA-matched platelets for platelet refractoriness
  • Irradiated platelets in immunocompromised patients

Plasma (FFP / FP24) Plasma Component

Plasma contains coagulation factors and is used to correct coagulation factor deficiencies.

Indications
  • Coagulopathy with active bleeding
  • Massive transfusion
  • Urgent reversal of warfarin (if PCC unavailable)
  • Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) – plasma exchange
Important Notes
  • AB plasma is universal donor
  • Requires ABO compatibility
  • Typical dose: 10–15 mL/kg

Cryoprecipitate Factor Concentrate

Cryoprecipitate is derived from thawed plasma and contains high concentrations of specific coagulation factors.

Key Components
  • Fibrinogen
  • Factor VIII
  • Factor XIII
  • von Willebrand factor
  • Fibronectin
Indications
  • Hypofibrinogenemia (fibrinogen <1.5–2.0 g/L)
  • Massive hemorrhage
  • Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)
  • Inherited fibrinogen deficiency

Granulocytes Cellular Component

Granulocyte transfusions provide temporary neutrophil support in patients with severe neutropenia and life-threatening infections.

Indications
  • Severe neutropenia (<0.5 ×10⁹/L)
  • Life-threatening bacterial or fungal infection
  • Failure of antimicrobial therapy
  • Expected marrow recovery
Special Considerations
  • Must be irradiated
  • ABO compatibility preferred
  • Short shelf life (~24 hours)
