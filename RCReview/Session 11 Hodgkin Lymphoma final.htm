What are Hodgkin Lymphomas?

●        Lymphoid neoplasms usually affecting lymph nodes.

●        Composed of large dysplastic mononuclear and multi-nucleated cells

●        Surrounded by a polymorphous mixture of mature non-neoplastic inflammatory cells (PCs, eos, lymphs & histiocytes)

What are the two major types of Hodgkin Lymphoma?

●        1. Nodular Lymphocyte predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma (NLPHL)

●        2. Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma (CHL)

What is the difference between NLPHL and CHL?

●        NLPHL has preserved B-cell phenotype

●        May have partial loss of B-cell phenotype

What percentage of HL is CHL?

●        10% NLPHL

What are Reed Sternberg Cells?

●        Large cells

●        Slightly basophilic cytoplasm

●        2+ nuclear lobes/nuclei (binucleated)

●        Large, rounded and prominent nuclei with some irregular membrane

●        Chromatin pale

●        Prominent eosinophilic nucleolus (RS cell usually has one in each lobe or nucleus - typical Owl's Eye)

●        Peri-nuclear clearing (halo)

 

Variants of Reed-Sternberg cell (RSC)(5) :

  1. Hodgkin's cell (atypical mononuclear Reed-Sternberg cell (RSC)
  2. Lacunar Reed-Sternberg cell (RSC)
  3. Pleomorphic Reed-Sternberg cell (RSC)
  4. Limfo-histiocytic ("pop-corn") variant
  5. "Mummy" Reed-Sternberg cell (RSC)

Hodgkin's lymphoma (H&E, ob.x20)
Typical Reed-Sternberg cell : size between 20 - 50 microns; abundant, amphofilic, finely granular/homogenous cytoplasm; two mirror-image nuclei ("owl eyes") each with an eosinophilic nucleolus and a thick nuclear membrane (chromatin is distributed on the inner surface of the nuclear membrane, generating a halo image around the nucleolus).

Hodgkin's lymphoma. Variants of Reed-Sternberg cell (H&E, ob.x20) :

  • Hodgkin's cell (atypical mononuclear Reed-Sternberg cell is a variant of Reed-Sternberg cell, which has the same characteristics, but is mononucleated
  • Lacunar Reed-Sternberg cell is large, with a single hyperlobated nucleus, multiple, small nucleoli and eosinophilic cytoplasm which is retracted around the nucleus, creating an empty space ("lacunae")
  • Pleomorphic Reed-Sternberg cell has multiple irregular nuclei
  • "Pop-corn" Reed-Sternberg cell (lympho-histiocytic variant) is a small cell, with a very lobulated nucleus, small nucleoli.
  • "Mummy" Reed-Sternberg cell has a compact nucleus, no nucleolus and basophilic cytoplasm

●        Mononuclear variants of R-S cells

Nodular Sclerosis classic Hodgkin Lymphoma. (White Book)

 

Define Classical Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL)?

●        A lymphoid neoplasm composed of Hodgkin and Reed-Sternberg (HRS) cells in a variable inflammatory background

Define Nodular Sclerosis Hodgkin Lymphoma (NSCHL)?

●        Most common type of CHL composed of lacunar-type HRS cells and inflammatory cells forming nodules surrounded by fibrous bands

●        Fibrous bands are collagen-rich, polarizable, and surround at least 1 nodule

Key histological characteristics of NSCHL?

●        Nodules

●        Collagenous and sclerotic bands that are polarizable and surround at least 1 nodule

●        Lacunar cells (type of Hodgkin cell) with fewer Reed-Sternberg cells

What is the name for the common neoplastic cells seen in NSCHL?

●        Lacunar cells - (type of Hodgkin cell) with fewer Reed-Sternberg cells

Describe the characteristics of the fibrous bands seen in NSCHL?

●        Collagen-rich

●        Polarizable

●        Surround at least 1 nodule

Why are the HRS cells in NSCHL called "lacunar cells"?

●        Because of retraction artifact of the cytoplasm causing the HRS cells to appears as an empty lacunar space.

●        Hence, these cells are known as lacunar cells

What Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma, Nodular Sclerosis type, histological variant is shown here?

 

Answer:

●        Syncytial variant

●        Lacunar cells form cohesive sheets (seen here as lighter staining areas in lymphoid nodules)

○        Confluent aggregates or cohesive sheets of lacunar cells

○        Cohesive appearance

●        Limited number of birefringent collagen bands and occasional necrosis

●        When this feature is pronounced, these cases may be mistakenly diagnosed as non-Hodgkin lymphoma, metastatic carcinoma, or even thymoma

What does "syncytial" mean?

●        "Together", "containing many", cytoplasmic continuity, not separated, confluent, cohesive

What is the histological distinction between CHL, NS syncytial variant and CHL, lymphocyte depleted??

●        Presence of absence of nodules

●        NS syncytial variant CHL: NODULES present

●        Lymphocyte depleted CHL: NO NODULES

Hodgkin and Reed-Sternberg (HRS) cells arise from what cells?

●        RS+H cells are of B-cell lineage with defects in B-cell differentiation

●        Arise from late germinal center B cells (MUM1 pos, BOB-1 and OCT-2 neg)

●        Ig gene rearrangements positive; many defects in B-cell differentiation

What % of CHL is NSCHL?

●        Represents ~ 70% of CHL cases in developed countries

●        ie most common type of CHL

NSCHL - Age group affected?

●        15-34 years; mediastinal or cervical lymph nodes

NSCHL Clinical Issues - Treatment modalities and effectiveness?

●        Current chemotherapy (ABVD) ± radiation *can cure* many patients

4 Top *Differential Diagnoses* for NSCHL?

  1. Primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma
  2. B-cell lymphoma, unclassifiable, with features intermediate between DLBCL and CHL
  3. Lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma
  4. Nodular lymphocyte predominant HL

Nodular Lymphocyte Predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma

NEW WHO

WHO-HAEM5 continues to list nodular lymphocyte predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) under the family of Hodgkin lymphoma; the existing terminology of NLPHL (Hodgkin lymphoma) is maintained so as not to interfere with ongoing clinical trials. However, NLPHL may be more accurately called “nodular lymphocyte predominant B-cell lymphoma” since the neoplastic cells have a functional B-cell program, and therefore this term is now considered acceptable in preparation of future definitive adoption of the new nomenclature.

An important issue in NLPHL is the recognition of the different growth patterns overlapping with T-cell/histiocyte-rich large B-cell lymphoma (THRLBCL) at the extreme end (Table 6). These patterns occur across all age groups. Some variant patterns (patterns C, D and E) have been associated with more aggressive clinical behaviour in retrospective analyses and may thus reflect the natural development and progression of the tumour.

In some cases, a clear distinction between NLPHL Pattern E and THRLBCL may not be possible since both diseases present with advanced clinical stage. Distinction is especially difficult on small biopsies, which may not be representative.

NEW ICC

The CAC conference discussed key issues related to the classification of Hodgkin lymphomas and patients with borderline diagnostic criteria. The conference concluded that new terminology is warranted for nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL), based on major biological and clinical differences with CHL and with close relationship to T-cell/histiocyte-rich large B-cell lymphoma.198 The term “nodular lymphocyte predominant B-cell lymphoma” (NLPBL) was accepted by consensus. The value of identifying variant histology in NLPBL was recognized, with the suggestion that typical patients with Fan patterns A, B, and C or grade 1 be distinguished from Fan patterns D, E, and F or grade 2.199 Patients falling within grade 2 generally show loss of a well-formed nodular pattern and increased infiltration by T cells with a reduction of background small B cells. Patients with grade 2 histology may warrant treatment for DLBCL, but clinical features should play a role in treatment decisions.200 Rare examples of NLPBL are EBV-positive with uncertain clinical implications.201

NLPHL immunophenotype of LP cells

●        B cells with preserved “B cell program” (unlike the weird HRS cells of cHL)

●        CD45/LCA(+), CD79a(+)

●        CD20(+), CD22(+), pax-5(+)

●        Bcl-6(+), OCT2(+), BOB1(+)

●        CD15(-), CD30(-), EBV(-), Bcl-2(-), MUM1(-)

●        LP cells carry monoclonal IgH gene rearrangements

●        Note: background small lymphocytes are also B cells [CD20(+), CD22(+), CD79a(+), CD75(+)] rather than T cells as in CHL or T cell Histiocyte rich large B cell lymphoma, both of which are key DDx considerations

NLPHL - Describe the typical Microscopic Pathology

●        Nodular or nodular and diffuse patterns or predominantly diffuse

●        LP cells scattered in background of small lymphocytes and histiocytes

●        LP cells have vesicular chromatin, inconspicuous nucleolus, and thin nuclear membrane

●        Mixture of different cell types gives "moth-eaten" pattern on low-power view

●        No necrosis or thick fibrous bands

●        Uninvolved lymph node shows reactive follicular hyperplasia &/or PTGC

NLPHL Single-cell PCR - LP cells carry what gene rearrangement?

●        Monoclonal *IgH gene* rearrangements
