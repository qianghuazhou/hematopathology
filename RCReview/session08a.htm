What chromosome is CALR on? What is the full name for CALR?      

 

●      Chromosomal location: 19p13.13

●      Approved name: Calreticulin

●      80% of mutations are 1 of 2 types

○      Type 1: 52-bp deletion: eliminates almost all of the negatively charged amino acids -> higher risk, more in overt PMF

○      Type 2: 5-bp insertion: retains approximately half of the negatively charged amino acids

○      Others: Type 1 like or Type 2 like, according to the extent of residual negatively charged amino acids

●      Mutations in CALR were identified in 2013 and found in 25–35% of patients with ET and PMF, but not in patients with PV.

●      CALR frameshift mutations are all predicted to result in a novel C-terminal protein sequence, the commonest being 52 bp deletion (type 1) or 5 bp insertion (type 2). These are found at similar frequencies in ET; type 1 mutations are much more common than type 2 mutations in PMF.

What are 4 characteristic findings [QZ1] of the BM histology seen in CML CP? 

1.     Hypercellular marrow

2.     Marked granulocytic proliferation at all stages of differentiation.

3.     Erythroid precursors are typically decreased.

4.     Megakaryocytes are often increased in number, with altered morphology, including small size and hyposegmented nuclei ("dwarf megakaryocytes").

5.     Eosinophils and basophils are increased.

6.     Pseudo-Gaucher cells are commonly seen.

7.     Reticulin fibrosis may be present in up to 30% of cases but does not correlate with response to imatinib therapy.

WHO 2022 Diagnostic criteria for PV? 

 

●      Diagnosis requires → All 3 major OR 1st 2 major and 1 minor

Major criteria:

1.   Elevated haemoglobin concentration (> 16.5 g/dL in men, > 16.0 g/dL in women) or elevated haematocrit (> 49%a in men, > 48% in women)

2.   Bone marrow biopsy showing age-adjusted hypercellularity with trilineage growth (panmyelosis), including prominent erythroid, granulocytic, and megakaryocytic proliferation with pleomorphic, mature megakaryocytes (differences in size)b

3.   Presence of JAK2 p.V617F or JAK2 exon 12 mutation

Minor criterion:

·    Subnormal serum erythropoietin level

The diagnosis of polycythaemia vera requires either all three major criteria or the first two major criteria plus the minor criterion

 


Diagnostic criteria for post-PV MF?     

 

●      Diagnosis requires → the 2 “required” PLUS 2 (out of 4) “additional” criteria  .

Required criteria:

·   Documentation of a previous diagnosis of WHO-defined polycythaemia vera

·   Bone marrow fibrosis of grade 2–3 on a scale of 0–3

Additional criteria (two are required):

·   Anaemia (below the reference range, given age, sex, and altitude considerations) or sustained loss of requirement of either phlebotomy (in the absence of cytoreductive therapy) or cytoreductive treatment for erythrocytosis

·   Leukoerythroblastosis

·   Increasing splenomegaly, defined as either an increase in palpable splenomegaly of > 50 mm from baseline (distance from the left costal margin) or the development of a newly palpable splenomegaly

·   Development of any two (or all three) of the following constitutional symptoms: > 10% weight loss in 6 months, night sweats, unexplained fever (> 37.5 °C)

CNL diagnostic criteria WHO2022 (5)?

1.     Peripheral blood WBC count ≥ 25 [QZ2] × 109/L; segmented neutrophils plus banded neutrophils constitute ≥ 80% of the WBCs; neutrophil precursors (promyelocytes, myelocytes, and metamyelocytes) constitute < 10% of the WBCs; myeloblasts are rarely observed; monocytes constitute < 10% of the peripheral blood leukocytes; absolute monocytosis not meeting criteria for chronic mye­lomonocytic leukaemia; no dysgranulopoiesis

2.     Hypercellular bone marrow; neutrophil granulocytes increased in percentage and number; neutrophil maturation appears normal; myeloblasts constitute < 5% of the nucleated cells

3.     Not meeting diagnostic criteria for chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML), polycythaemia vera, essential thrombocythaemia, or primary myelofibrosis, and exclusion of reactive neutrophilia

4.     No evidence of disease-defining gene rearrangements such as in PDGFRA, PDGFRB, or FGFR1, and no PCM1::JAK2 fusion

5.     Presence of CSF3R p.T618I or another activating CSF3R mutation

6.     OR persistent neutrophilia (≥ 3 months), splenomegaly, and no identifiable cause of reactive neutrophilia including absence of a plasma cell neoplasm or, if a plasma cell neoplasm is present, demonstration of clonality of myeloid cells by cytogenetic or molecular studies

ET - Essential thrombocythemia

 

Essential thrombocythemia, WHO 2022 Diagnostic Criteria?

 

●      4 major criteria

●      2 minor

●      Diagnosis requires either → all 4 major criteria OR the 1st 3 major plus 1 minor criteria

    

Major criteria:

Platelet count ≥ 450 × 109/L

Bone marrow biopsy showing proliferation mainly of the megakaryocytic lineage, with increased numbers of enlarged, mature megakaryocytes with hyperlobulated nuclei; no significant increase or left shift in neutrophil granulopoiesis or erythropoiesis; very rarely a minor (grade 1) increase in reticulin fibres

WHO criteria for BCR::ABL1-positive chronic myeloid leukaemia, polycythaemia vera, primary myelofibrosis, and other myeloid neoplasms are not met

JAK2, CALR, or MPL mutation

Minor criteria:

Presence of a clonal marker

OR

Exclusion of reactive thrombocytosis

The diagnosis of essential thrombocythaemia requires either all the major criteria or the first three major criteria plus a minor criterion to be met.

 

 

Essential Thrombocythemia Post ET Myelofibrosis WHO 2022 Criteria?     

 

●      Diagnosis requires → the 2 “required” criteria PLUS 2 (out of 5) “additional” criteria.

Required criteria:

Documentation of a previous diagnosis of WHO-defined essential thrombocythaemia

Bone marrow fibrosis of grade 2–3 on a scale of 0–3

Additional criteria:

Anaemia (below the reference range, given age, sex, and altitude considerations) and a > 2 g/dL decrease from baseline haemoglobin concentration

Leukoerythroblastosis

Increasing splenomegaly, defined as either an increase in palpable splenomegaly of > 50 mm from baseline (distance from the left costal margin, or on imaging) or the development of newly palpable splenomegaly

Elevated LDH level (above the reference range)

Development of any two (or all three) of the following constitutional symptoms: > 10% weight loss in 6 months, night sweats, unexplained fever (> 37.5 °C)

The diagnosis of post-ET MF requires either both of the required criteria or at least two additional criteria.

CEL, NOS

What is the definition of hypereosinophilia - Blood and Tissue?

 

●      Hypereosinophilia (HE) is defined as peripheral blood eosinophilia > 1.5 × 109/L on at least two occasions over an interval of at least 4 weeks, but it is recognized that some cases with life-threatening organ damage may require urgent therapy without waiting for confirmation of sustained eosinophilia (WHO2022)

●      Tissue HE [QZ3] 



What is excluded in the diagnosis of CEL, NOS? List 7 specific clonal abnormalities that must be absent   

 

Excludes cases positive for:

 

●      BCR-ABL1, PDGFRA, PDGFRB, FGFR1, PCM1-JAK2, ETV6-JAK2, BCR-JAK2 fusion

●      inv(16),t(16;16), t(8;21)

What is the eosinophil cutoff in CEL, NOS 

 

●      Eosinophils >1.5 x 10^9/L in PB

●      Should be clonal myeloid cells in PB or BM (although hard to prove)

●      Without an increase in blast cells, these get classified as Hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES)

List 5 Clinical Features of CEL, NOS      

 

- Rare; 70th decade of life

Asymptomatic cases:

  • Eosinophilia detected incidentally.

Constitutional symptoms:

  • Weight loss
  • Night sweats
  • Fever

Other symptoms:

  • Fatigue
  • Cough
  • Angio-oedema
  • Muscle pain
  • Pruritus (itching)
  • Diarrhea

Severe complications:

  • Endomyocarditis leading to:
    • Endomyocardial fibrosis
    • Restrictive cardiomyopathy
    • Valvular regurgitation (mitral and tricuspid valves)
    • Intracardiac thrombi (which may embolize to brain or other organs)
    • Cardiac failure
  • Neurological and pulmonary involvement:
    • Peripheral neuropathy
    • CNS dysfunction
    • Pulmonary symptoms (due to lung infiltration)


What is the most severe clinical consequence of CEL, NOS?      

 

●      Most severe = endomyocardial fibrosis with restrictive cardiomegaly, cardiac failure

●      Scarring of mitral/tricuspid valdes leads to regurg and thrombi

What is Idiopathic Hypereosinophilic syndrome (IHES)?
- Required Eo count? What needs to be excluded? Is there a clonal abnormality? Is there end organ damage?      

 

●      1. Diagnosis of exclusion

○      HES = Eosinophilia >6 months > or = 1.5 x 10^9/L in PB

●      2. Reactive causes excluded

●      3. Myeloid neoplasms excluded (AML, MPN, MDS/MPN, SM)

●      4. Cytokine-producing immunophenotypically aberrant T-cell population excluded

○      Lymphocytic variant HES (Release of cytokines by aberrant T cells that may be clonal)

●      5. Tissue damage due to eosinophilia

○      If no end organ damage = idiopathic hypereosinophilia (IHE)

●      Note: May include some causes of CEL, NOS → f/u necessary

What is the difference between idiopathic hypereosinophilic syndrome and idiopathic hypereosinophilia?      

 

●      The syndrome requires END ORGAN DAMAGE

 

What is the classic immunophenotype we look for in L-HES?

 

●      Lymphocytic-variant HES

○      Mix of clonal and reactive processes

○      Clonal T cell population with abnormal phenotype

○      Not picked up on routine LPD panel

■      CD4+3- (for sure!!)

■      CD3+CD4+CD7−

■      CD3+CD4−CD8− TCRαβ+

○      Secrete growth factors (mainly IL-5) leading to reactive eosinophilia

○      About 50% have T cell clonality by PCR

○      Often have cutaneous manifestations

○      Predilection to T-cell lymphoma

 

What are the other variants of HES?

 

·       Myeloid variants of HES (M-HES): Display features of MPN: splenomegaly, high serum B12, chromosomal abnormalities, anemia, TCP and left-shifted granulopoiesis 

  • T lymphocytic variants of HES (L-HES)
  • Familial HES: AD, 5q31-33. Can be Asymptomatic with eosinophilia begins at birth. Single-organ HES variant can be seen
  • Idiopathic HES (IHES)
  • Organ-restricted hypereosinophilic conditions – This term is applied to disorders with blood eosinophilia ≥1500/microliter in the setting of single organ involvement.
  • Specific/defined syndromes associated with HE – This term is applied to disorders with blood eosinophilia ≥1500/microliter in association with a distinct diagnosis, wherein the role of eosinophils in disease complications and clinical manifestations remains inconclusive.

Essential: 

·       Hypereosinophilia, defined as peripheral blood eosinophilia > 1.5 × 109/L on at least two occasions over an interval of at least 4 weeks;

·       evidence of clonality;

·       abnormal bone marrow morphology;

·       not meeting diagnostic criteria for other myeloid or lymphoid neoplasms.

MPN-U - Myeloproliferative Neoplasm, Unclassifiable

 

How do we distinguish between MPN, U and reactive causes? (Give 5 specific examples of mutations)      

 

●      Extended molecular testing can help establish diagnosis of MPN (vs. reactive)

●      TET2, IDH1, IDH2, EZH2, → ASXL1, SRSF2, SF3B1

○      Mnemonic = TIE, ASS!

○      Although these may also occur in healthy elderly

What are the 3 MPN, U subgroups?      

 

1. Early MPN group
-Masked/Pre-polycythemic presentation of PV, Prefibrotic/early PMF, or early phase ET
-Where characteristic features not yet developed

2. Late (Advanced stage) MPN with pronounced myelofibrosis, osteosclerosis or transformation to a more aggressive stage with increased blast counts and/or MDS → obscure the underlying disorder

3. Convincing evidence of MPN in which coexisting neoplastic or inflammatory disorder → obscures the usual diagnostic clinical and morphological features
 
Myeloproliferative Neoplasm, Unclassifiable Criteria (3 criteria)(WHO2022)?

 

Requires all three of the following:

Presence of features of any one of the following features:

·       Clinical and haematological features of an MPN (e.g. splenomegaly, leukocytosis, thrombocytosis) in the absence of significant monocytosis and significant eosinophilia

·       Bone marrow hypercellularity with megakaryocytic hyperplasia and varying degrees of granulocytic and erythroid hyperplasia, without dysplastic features

·       Clinical and morphological features can be discrepanta

Not meeting criteria for any other MPN, MDS, MDS/MPNb, or myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase gene fusions

Presence of driver mutations such as JAK2, CALR, or MPL mutations, or another clonal markerc

Requires the absence of both of the following:

Insufficient clinical data or inadequate bone marrow specimen for accurate evaluation and classification

Recent history of cytotoxic or growth factor therapy, particularly when dysplastic features are seen

 

 

When is it inappropriate to use MPN, U category - give 2 examples      

 

Inappropriate to use if:

 

●      Clinical data not sufficient/unavailable

●      Bone marrow specimen inadequate

●      Patient received cytotoxic or growth factor therapy

What is the Differential Diagnosis for MPN,U - Give 4 alternative causes      

 

●      If features of one of the well-defined MPNs are not present → must entertain the possibility of reactive cause  = infection, inflammatory, toxins, chemo, growth factors etc.

●      Hematological and non-hematological malignancies causing reactive changes

●      Cytopenias/ dysplasia may better fit with MDS/MPN
