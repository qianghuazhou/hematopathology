Sydney / WHO Category Language

L1 Nondiagnostic: repeat FNA with ROSE, core/excision if clinically indicated.
L2 Benign: clinical/radiologic correlation; repeat if persistent or discordant.
L3 AUS/ALUS: atypical but insufficient; acquire material for flow/cell block/core.
L4 Suspicious: biopsy and ancillary studies recommended.
L5 Malignant: classify with flow, IHC, molecular, and tissue correlation.

Body Fluid / CSF Approach

Ask first: Is this a hematologic question, carcinoma question, infection question, or therapy surveillance?

Screen: cellularity, preservation, epithelial clusters, mesothelial cells, lymphoid population, plasma cells, blasts, macrophages, organisms, necrosis.

Then integrate: cytology + flow + cell block/IHC + prior/concurrent tissue.

CSF Notes

Low cellularity is common; interpret with caution.
Flow can detect low-level involvement, but correlation with cytospin morphology is important.
If very few lymphoid cells are present, say: “A small number of lymphoid cells is present; flow cytometry is recommended/performed for further evaluation.”

BAL / Respiratory Cytology Approach

1. Adequacy and specimen quality
Macrophages, bronchial cells, blood, squamous contamination.

2. Infection/inflammation
Neutrophilic, eosinophilic, granulomatous, fungal/viral inclusions.

3. Malignancy screen
Atypical epithelial cells vs lymphoma/leukemia involvement.

4. Ancillary
Microbiology, special stains, flow when hematologic involvement is suspected, cell block/IHC when atypical cells are present.

Useful BAL Report Phrases

Negative for malignant cells. Alveolar macrophages and bronchial epithelial cells are present.
Acute inflammation is present. Correlation with microbiologic studies is recommended.
Atypical cells are present; the findings are limited by degeneration/inflammation. Correlation with imaging and repeat sampling or tissue biopsy is recommended if clinically indicated.

Heme-Specific BAL Pitfalls

Flow may identify circulating blood contamination rather than true pulmonary involvement.
Large lymphoma cells may be poorly represented by flow; cell block/IHC correlation is useful.
In immunosuppressed patients, infection and lymphoma can coexist.

Flow Correlation Rules

Concordant positive: morphology and flow identify the same abnormal population; report as integrated involvement.
Flow positive, morphology subtle: report low-level involvement if immunophenotype is convincing; mention limited morphologic correlate.
Morphology suspicious, flow negative: consider large cell lymphoma, HL, ALCL, necrosis, poor viability, sampling, fragile cells, or non-hematolymphoid malignancy.
Flow negative, polymorphous cytology: supports reactive process but does not exclude focal/partial lymphoma or HL.

Flow-Limited Comment

Flow cytometry does not identify an abnormal lymphoid population. However, correlation with cytomorphology and tissue biopsy is required, as some processes, including Hodgkin lymphoma, large cell lymphoma, necrotic tumors, and focal/partial involvement, may be underrepresented or not detected by flow cytometry.

Reference Backbone

This tool is built around the practical workflow emphasized in lymph node FNA literature: stepwise cytomorphology, flow cytometry, immunocytochemistry/cell block, and integrated reporting.

  • Stepwise lymph node FNA approach: clinical context → low-power pattern → high-power pattern → disease-specific clues → reporting category.
  • FNA + flow cytometry: especially useful for small B-cell processes and objective detection of clonal populations, but limited in Hodgkin lymphoma, large cell lymphoma, necrotic lesions, and low-viability samples.
  • Immunocytochemistry/cell block: critical for lineage confirmation, carcinoma vs lymphoma, HL/ALCL/THRLBCL, and large B-cell lymphoma workup.
  • Concurrent biopsy correlation: prioritized because many daily specimens are verification/correlation cases rather than stand-alone cytology diagnoses.
