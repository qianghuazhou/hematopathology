Intravenous Immune Globulin (IVIG)

Overview

Intravenous immune globulin (IVIG) is a manufactured blood product prepared from pooled human plasma obtained from several thousand donors. It contains immunoglobulin proteins, predominantly IgG, and undergoes multiple viral inactivation and purification steps.

What IVIG does

  • Provides passive antibody replacement in immune deficiency states.
  • Acts as an immunomodulatory therapy in selected inflammatory and autoimmune disorders.

Proposed mechanisms

  • Fc receptor blockade
  • Cytokine suppression
  • Complement modulation
  • Neutralization of toxins
  • Effects on regulatory T cells and immune cell maturation
The dominant mechanism likely varies by disease, and it remains unclear whether all IgG molecules or only a subset are responsible for the clinical benefit.

Indications

IVIG should be prescribed for diagnoses supported by institutional approval pathways and provincial or national utilization guidelines rather than by serum IgG level alone or by broad disease category alone.

Use guideline-based approval. In Ontario practice, this may be based on the Ontario Immune Globulin Utilization Management Guidelines or the Prairie Collaborative Criteria for the Clinical Use of Immune Globulin.

Example: Toxic shock syndrome

For suspected or confirmed toxic shock syndrome, IVIG is recommended as an adjunct to source control, antibiotic therapy, and other supportive measures.

Clinical setting Recommendation Example dose options
Toxic shock syndrome Use in addition to surgery, antibiotics, and supportive care; infectious diseases consultation is strongly recommended. 2 g/kg as a single dose, or 1 g/kg on day 1 and 0.5 g/kg on days 2 and 3

Dose calculation

IVIG dosing varies by indication. Typical dose ranges are 0.3 g/kg to 2 g/kg, with some exceptions such as up to 3 g/kg for toxic epidermal necrolysis / Stevens–Johnson syndrome.

Adult dosing weight

For adults with height at least 152.4 cm and BMI at least 30 kg/m², dosing may be based on ideal body weight or adjusted body weight, depending on local policy.

Why adjusted dosing matters

  • Reduces risk of toxicity from unnecessarily high doses
  • Supports stewardship of a limited blood product
  • May reduce cost and waste
Infusion rate formula:
Rate (mL/kg/hr) × Weight (kg) = Hourly infusion rate (mL/hr)

In chronic IVIG-treated disease, once the patient is stable, the dose and/or treatment interval should be titrated to the lowest effective dose and greatest effective interval where appropriate.

Administration

Although IVIG brands are considered clinically equivalent in effectiveness, each brand has its own monograph-defined infusion rates, cautions, and maximum recommended rates.

Brand-specific infusion rates matter. Start at the manufacturer-recommended initial rate and escalate only if the patient tolerates the infusion.

Before starting

  • Confirm informed consent
  • Verify indication and dose
  • Use aseptic technique
  • Ensure prescriber support is available

Line and tubing

  • Use a dedicated IV line
  • Peripheral or central access is acceptable
  • Do not mix with other medications
  • Use standard vented tubing

Product checks

  • Seal must be intact
  • Solution should be clear or slightly opalescent
  • Colour should be colourless to pale yellow
  • Do not infuse if appearance is concerning

Handling points

  • Complete each bottle within 4 hours of puncturing the seal.
  • Do not shake the bottle.
  • Allow product to reach room temperature to reduce bubbling.
  • Start with the smallest bottle and finish with the largest to minimize wastage if a reaction occurs.
  • Use an approved infusion pump for precise rate control and patient mobility.

Flush compatibility

  • Most brands: D5W or 0.9% NaCl
  • Exception: Gammagard S/D should be flushed with D5W only
Different IVIG brands should not be infused in the same infusion. The presentation notes an exception for Gamunex and IGIVnex.

Monitoring

Monitor patients closely during infusion, especially at initiation and after each rate increase.

Time point Recommended assessment
30 minutes before infusion Baseline patient assessment and vital signs
15 minutes after start Reassess vital signs and tolerance
After each rate increase Assess symptoms and vital signs
Hourly until completion Ongoing monitoring
After completion Inpatients: 1-hour post-infusion; outpatients: reassess before discharge
If a reaction is suspected, stop the infusion, maintain IV access, check vital signs, notify the prescriber, provide care as ordered, and report the event to the transfusion medicine laboratory, even if minor.

Adverse effects and cautions

Common / usually minor

  • Headache
  • Fever or chills
  • Flushing
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Rash, hives, pruritus
  • Myalgias

Moderate to severe

  • Anaphylaxis / anaphylactoid reaction
  • Aseptic meningitis
  • Hemolysis / hemolytic anemia
  • Renal dysfunction or acute renal failure
  • TACO
  • TRALI (rare)
  • Thromboembolic events

Risk factors for adverse reactions

  • First IVIG exposure
  • Change to another brand
  • More than 8 weeks since previous infusion
  • High dose
  • Rapid infusion rate
  • Poor hydration

Delayed symptoms to report

Some reactions can occur up to 72 hours after infusion, and signs of hemolysis may occur up to 10 days later.

  • Shortness of breath
  • Chest, back, or abdominal pain
  • Palpitations
  • Severe headache or neck stiffness
  • Dark red, brown, or tea-coloured urine
  • Jaundice or scleral icterus
IVIG can interfere with the effectiveness of live attenuated vaccines such as measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella for approximately 6 to 12 weeks.

Strategies to reduce toxicity

  • Use the slowest feasible infusion rate.
  • Ensure the patient is well hydrated before and after treatment.
  • Consider dividing high doses, especially doses greater than 1 g/kg, over more than one day.
  • Assess for risk factors for renal dysfunction, thrombosis, and circulatory overload before infusion.
  • Obtain baseline laboratory studies when hemolysis risk is relevant and monitor clinically afterward.

Practical blood bank points

Stewardship

  • IVIG is a limited blood product and should be guideline-supported.
  • Adjusted or ideal body weight dosing may reduce overuse.
  • Maintenance regimens should be titrated to the lowest effective dose where possible.

Operational details

  • Standard vented tubing is usually sufficient.
  • Inline filter generally not required, except brand-specific situations such as Gammagard S/D.
  • Subsequent bottles in the same treatment do not require returning to the initial starting rate.
