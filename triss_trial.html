TRISS Trial

Lower versus Higher Hemoglobin Threshold for Transfusion in Septic Shock

TRISS Trials Group. New England Journal of Medicine. 2014;371:1381-1391.

Clinical Question

In patients with septic shock, does transfusion using a lower haemoglobin threshold compared with a higher threshold improve mortality?

Study Design

  • Randomised controlled trial
  • Stratified by:
    • Study site
    • Presence of active haematological malignancy
  • Permuted blocks of varying sizes
  • Blinding of investigators assessing the primary outcome
  • Blinding of the trial statistician
  • Intention-to-treat analysis

Power Calculation

  • Assumed mortality in higher-threshold group: 45%
  • Sample size of 1000 patients
  • Designed to detect a 9% absolute risk reduction
  • False positive rate: 5%
  • False negative rate: 20%

Setting

  • 32 general intensive care units
  • Scandinavia
  • December 2011 – December 2013

Population

Inclusion Criteria

  • Adult ICU patients
  • Septic shock
  • Hemoglobin ≤9 g/dL

Exclusion Criteria

  • Previous serious transfusion reaction
  • Prior RBC transfusion during ICU stay
  • Acute coronary syndrome
  • Life-threatening bleeding
  • Acute burn injury
  • Withdrawal from active treatment

Primary outcome data were obtained for 998 of 1005 patients. Baseline characteristics were similar between groups.

Baseline Characteristics

Surgery During Index Hospitalisation

  • Emergency surgery: 38% vs 43.8%
  • Elective surgery: 11.8% vs 10.7%

Source of Sepsis

  • Lung: 53.2% vs 52.2%
  • Abdomen: 41% vs 39.9%
  • Urinary tract: 11.6% vs 12.3%
  • Soft tissue: 11.8% vs 11.9%
  • Other: 10% vs 9.5%
  • Chronic cardiovascular disease: 14.9% vs 13.3%
  • Positive culture: 37.5% vs 32.3%

Intervention

Transfusion threshold of ≤7 g/dL.

Control

Transfusion threshold of ≤9 g/dL.

Transfusion Protocol

  • Single unit crossmatched RBC transfusions
  • Pre-storage leukoreduced red cells
  • Transfusion when hemoglobin reached assigned threshold
  • Intervention period: entire ICU stay (maximum 90 days)
  • Physicians allowed to transfuse outside protocol if:
    • Life-threatening bleeding
    • Ischemia
    • Need for ECMO

Primary Outcome

Death by 90 days: No significant difference between groups.

Secondary Outcomes

  • Median number of RBC transfusions – higher in liberal group
  • Proportion of patients avoiding transfusion – higher in restrictive group
  • No significant difference in:
    • Life support use at days 5, 14, 28
    • Days alive without:
      • Vasopressor or inotropic support
      • Mechanical ventilation
      • Renal replacement therapy
    • Days alive and out of hospital
    • Complications

Subgroup Analysis

No significant difference in mortality based on:
  • Presence vs absence of chronic cardiovascular disease
  • Age >70 vs ≤70
  • SAPS II score >53 vs ≤53

Protocol Suspensions and Violations

  • More transfusions above threshold in ≤7 g/dL group
  • More withholding of transfusion in ≤9 g/dL group

Authors' Conclusions

In patients with septic shock, mortality and rates of ischemic events were similar between those assigned to transfusion thresholds of 7 g/dL versus 9 g/dL.

Strengths

  • Randomized trial
  • Blinding of outcome assessors
  • Multicenter design

Weaknesses

  • Clinical staff were not blinded
  • Protocol violations may reduce ability to detect differences
  • However, a mean Hb difference of ~1.5 g/dL between groups remained
  • Per-protocol analysis confirmed similar results

Bottom Line

In ICU patients with septic shock, a transfusion threshold of 7 g/dL compared with 9 g/dL results in:
  • No difference in mortality
  • No increase in ischemic events
  • Reduced RBC transfusion use
Restrictive transfusion strategy is therefore safe in septic shock.

Patients with acute coronary syndrome were excluded, and further studies are required in this population.
