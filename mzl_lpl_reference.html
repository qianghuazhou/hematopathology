MZL and LPL Diagnostic Reference

A practical quick-reference page comparing marginal zone lymphoma (MZL) and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma (LPL), with emphasis on marrow and nodal pathology, clinicopathologic correlation, ancillary testing, and ready-to-use pathology templates.

Overview

The main day-to-day diagnostic problem is distinguishing MZL from LPL when a small B-cell lymphoma involves bone marrow and/or shows plasmacytic differentiation. The most useful practical anchors are marrow pattern, clinical setting, IgM paraprotein, and MYD88 p.L265P.

High-Yield Points

  • Paratrabecular and interstitial marrow pattern favors LPL
  • Nodular and intrasinusoidal pattern favors MZL
  • High IgM paraprotein and MYD88 positivity strongly support LPL
  • Follicular colonization and monocytoid cytology support MZL

Overview

MZL

When to favor MZL

  • Nodular and/or intrasinusoidal marrow involvement
  • Splenomegaly and peripheral blood lymphocytosis
  • Monocytoid cytology
  • Follicular colonization in nodal tissue
  • MYD88 usually negative
LPL

When to favor LPL

  • Paratrabecular and interstitial marrow involvement
  • IgM paraproteinemia / Waldenström setting
  • Plasmacytoid lymphocytes and plasma cells with shared light chain restriction
  • MYD88 p.L265P detected
  • Marrow-centered disease with compatible clinical context
Overlap

Common pitfall

  • Both can involve marrow and lymph node
  • Both can show plasmacytic differentiation
  • Both are usually CD5 negative and CD10 negative
  • No single histologic feature is absolutely specific
Practical framing: first decide whether the pattern and clinical setting are more compatible with a marrow-centered IgM-associated lymphoma (LPL) or a marginal zone process with nodal, splenic, or extranodal features (MZL).

Bone Marrow Features

Marrow features favoring MZL

  • Nodular and/or intrasinusoidal infiltrates
  • Often focal, sometimes mixed with interstitial areas
  • Small to intermediate lymphocytes with more monocytoid appearance
  • Correlation with splenomegaly and lymphocytosis is useful

Marrow features favoring LPL

  • Paratrabecular and interstitial infiltrates
  • Spectrum from small lymphocytes to plasmacytoid lymphocytes and plasma cells
  • Shared light chain restriction in B cells and plasma cells is very supportive
  • IgM paraprotein and MYD88 positivity materially strengthen the diagnosis
High-yield marrow shortcut:
Nodular / intrasinusoidal → think MZL
Paratrabecular / interstitial + IgM / MYD88 → think LPL

Lymph Node Features

MZL in node

  • Diffuse to vaguely nodular growth
  • Follicular colonization
  • Expanded and disrupted FDC meshworks
  • Small to intermediate atypical lymphocytes with monocytoid cytoplasm
  • Large cells scant, without sheets

LPL in node

  • Nodal disease can occur but is not the main defining pattern
  • Usually requires convincing plasmacytoid / plasma cell component
  • Interpret in the setting of marrow disease, IgM paraprotein, and MYD88
  • Without that context, nodal material alone may be difficult to classify confidently

Ancillary Testing

Study Most useful role
Flow cytometry Defines clonality and phenotype; particularly useful in minimally involved marrow
Light chain studies Support clonality; shared restriction in plasma cells is especially helpful for LPL
CD21 / CD23 Highlight follicular dendritic cell meshworks and follicular colonization in MZL
MYD88 p.L265P Major discriminator favoring LPL in the appropriate context
Serum protein studies IgM paraprotein strongly supports LPL / Waldenström spectrum

Diagnostic Algorithm

  1. Assess the specimen type and pattern: marrow pattern is often the most useful initial discriminator.
  2. Look at cytology: monocytoid versus plasmacytoid / plasma cell-rich.
  3. Review the immunophenotype: both are often CD5- / CD10-, so do not overinterpret a generic small B-cell phenotype.
  4. Check for follicular colonization: supportive for MZL.
  5. Check serum IgM and MYD88: these are among the strongest practical features favoring LPL.
  6. Integrate the whole picture: when overlap remains substantial, sign out conservatively and recommend clinicopathologic correlation.

Site-Based Interpretation

Do not start with MYD88

In day-to-day practice, the first question is usually not “is MYD88 positive?” but rather where is the disease and what is the architectural pattern? Site and growth pattern often provide the strongest initial diagnostic framework.

Extranodal site strongly matters

Classical extranodal mucosal sites such as conjunctiva, ocular adnexa, salivary gland, stomach, thyroid, and lung strongly bias the differential toward extranodal marginal zone lymphoma (MALT lymphoma). In such sites, plasmacytic differentiation alone should not push the diagnosis toward LPL.

Practical hierarchy

  1. Site and architecture
  2. Cytology and distribution of plasma cells
  3. Flow cytometry and light chain studies
  4. Serum protein studies
  5. MYD88 and other molecular data
Working rule: extranodal mucosal disease usually defaults toward MZL/MALT, while marrow-centered disease with IgM paraproteinemia and compatible mutation data favors LPL.

Conjunctival / MALT Pitfall

Why conjunctiva usually favors MZL

  • Conjunctiva is a classic site for extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of MALT type
  • Monocytoid morphology is helpful when present, but is not required
  • Plasmacytic differentiation can be seen in MALT lymphoma and is not specific for LPL
  • In this site, many pathologists are comfortable diagnosing MALT lymphoma even if the cytology is not perfectly classic

How to hedge when needed

  • If morphology and phenotype are limited, sign out as a low-grade B-cell lymphoma with plasmacytic differentiation favoring extranodal MZL
  • Reserve broader MZL versus LPL differential language for unusually plasmacytic cases or when systemic clinical data are strongly suggestive of LPL/WM
  • MYD88 and serum studies may help, but lack of either should not prevent a favored diagnosis in the right site
Important correction: absence of monocytoid cells does not exclude MALT lymphoma, and a positive MYD88 result would be supportive of LPL but would still require clinicopathologic interpretation rather than automatic reclassification.

Immunophenotype Phrase Bank

MZL phrasing

The atypical lymphoid cells are CD20+ and PAX5+ B-cells that express BCL2 and are negative for CD5, CD10, and cyclin D1. BCL6 is negative in the neoplastic cells, with staining confined to residual or colonized follicles. CD21 and/or CD23 highlight expanded and disrupted follicular dendritic cell meshworks. Kappa and lambda studies show [light chain restriction / no definite plasmacytic light chain restriction]. Ki-67 is [low / low overall with accentuation in colonized follicles].

LPL phrasing

The infiltrate is composed of small B-lymphocytes with admixed plasmacytoid lymphocytes and plasma cells. The atypical cells are CD20+ and PAX5+ B-cells with [kappa/lambda] light chain restriction. CD138 highlights plasma cells, which show the same light chain restriction. The neoplastic cells are negative for CD5, CD10, and cyclin D1. IgM is [positive / not assessed]. MYD88 p.L265P is [detected / not detected / pending].

Key Diagnostic Pearls

  • In marrow, pattern is often the most useful morphologic discriminator.
  • Follicular colonization is a highly practical clue in favor of MZL in nodal tissue.
  • MYD88 p.L265P is one of the strongest practical ancillary studies favoring LPL, but it is not perfectly specific.
  • High-level IgM paraprotein strongly supports LPL but does not entirely exclude MZL.
  • Plasmacytic differentiation is non-specific; it can be seen in both MZL and LPL.
  • Monocytoid morphology is helpful for MZL but is not required, especially at classic extranodal MALT sites.
  • Site matters: conjunctiva and other classic mucosal sites strongly bias toward extranodal MZL/MALT lymphoma.
  • Some cases remain best classified conservatively on limited material, with final classification deferred to integrated clinicopathologic review.
