Diagnostic essence

Pattern

Nodal RDD: preserved but distorted architecture with markedly dilated sinuses filled by pale histiocytes, lymphocytes and plasma cells.

Cell

Large histiocytes with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm, vesicular round/central nuclei, distinct nucleoli, and emperipolesis.

Phenotype

S100+, OCT-2+, CD68/CD163+, cyclin D1 and/or pERK often positive; CD1a-, CD207/langerin-.

Practical diagnostic line: RDD is favored when characteristic histiocytes with emperipolesis are present in a compatible pattern and show S100/OCT2 positivity with absence of CD1a/langerin. In extranodal lesions, emperipolesis may be focal or absent; the immunophenotype becomes more important.

Older term Sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy Current term Rosai-Dorfman disease / Rosai-Dorfman-Destombes disease

Morphology checklist

Nodal disease

  • Architecture often intact but distorted.
  • Marked sinus dilatation.
  • Sinuses filled by RDD histiocytes.
  • Emperipolesis of lymphocytes, plasma cells, neutrophils or RBCs.
  • Often many plasma cells between sinuses.
  • Reactive follicles may be present.
  • Sclerosis is common.
  • Mitotic figures are uncommon.

Extranodal disease

  • May form a mass in skin/subcutis, bone, head/neck, upper respiratory tract, kidney, breast, dura, or other sites.
  • Sinusoidal pattern is absent outside lymph node.
  • RDD histiocytes may be sparse.
  • Emperipolesis may be minimal or absent.
  • Fibrosis and plasma cells can be prominent.

Helpful cytology clue

Large histiocytes with abundant cytoplasm and well-defined borders, central nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and engulfed viable inflammatory cells. Early lesions may show many lymphocytes/immunoblasts; late lesions may show more plasma cells and Russell bodies.

IgG4-related disease overlap

Important pitfall

RDD can show increased IgG4-positive plasma cells. Increased IgG4 cells alone do not establish IgG4-related disease.

Typical IgG4-RD lymph node clue

IgG4-RD lymph nodes may show follicular hyperplasia, PTGC-like changes, interfollicular expansion, Castleman-like pattern, or inflammatory pseudotumor-like changes.

Practical approach: report the IgG4 count/ratio when increased, but emphasize correlation with serum IgG4, systemic organ involvement, storiform fibrosis/obliterative phlebitis in extranodal tissue, and exclusion of RDD when RDD histiocytes are present.

Differential diagnosis

Langerhans cell histiocytosis

Can expand sinuses. Grooved/folded nuclei, eosinophils and necrosis are common. S100+, CD1a+, langerin/CD207+; OCT2 negative. No true emperipolesis.

Erdheim-Chester disease

Extranodal disease with foamy histiocytes, Touton-like giant cells and fibrosis; long bone involvement common. CD68+, factor XIIIa+, usually S100-, CD1a-, langerin-, OCT2-.

IgG4-related disease

May show increased IgG4+ plasma cells and sclerosis, including Castleman-like lymph node pattern. No RDD histiocytes/emperipolesis.

Chronic granulomatous inflammation

Necrosis and mixed acute/chronic inflammation may be present. Epithelioid histiocytes, multinucleated giant cells; organisms may be identified. Histiocytes do not resemble RDD.

Histiocytic sarcoma

Architectural effacement, cytologic atypia and mitotic activity. Phagocytosis can occur but true emperipolesis is not prominent.

Metastatic melanoma/carcinoma

Sinusoidal metastasis can mimic sinus process. Look for cohesive atypical cells; melanoma markers or keratins support metastasis.

Anaplastic large cell lymphoma

May show sinusoidal involvement. Large atypical CD30+ cells, ALK positive or negative; S100 negative. Consider T-cell markers and TCR studies.

Classic Hodgkin lymphoma

Rarely sinus-predominant. HRS cells CD30+, CD15+/-, weak PAX5+, CD45-, S100-; eosinophils often present.

Toxoplasma lymphadenitis

Triad: follicular hyperplasia, monocytoid B-cell sinusoidal expansion, and epithelioid histiocytes encroaching on germinal centers.

Reactive sinus histiocytosis

Nonspecific sinus expansion by smaller benign histiocytes with less cytoplasm; no characteristic RDD cytology or emperipolesis; S100 negative or focal.

Suggested workup

Minimum diagnostic panel

S100OCT2CD68/CD163CD1aLangerin/CD207

Add keratin, SOX10/MART1/HMB45, CD30/ALK, EBER/HHV8, or infectious stains as dictated by morphology.

When to add molecular

Consider MAPK pathway testing in multifocal, recurrent, refractory, extranodal, or clinically aggressive disease, or when targeted therapy is being considered.

When plasma cells are prominent

Assess kappa/lambda, IgG/IgG4 if clinically relevant, and correlate with SPEP/IFE, serum free light chains, serum IgG4, autoimmune features and systemic disease.

Associated diseases

RDD can coexist with lymphoma or other histiocytic disorders. A focal RDD component should not distract from evaluation of a separate neoplasm.
